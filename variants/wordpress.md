## Before You Apply: What Approval Actually Depends On

The most common founder mistake is applying cold. Approval is not random, and it is not about your personal FICO score. Brex evaluates the business: how it is structured, how much cash it holds, and whether it looks like a real operating company. Before you touch the application, work through the readiness checklist below. The full detail lives in our breakdown of the [brex card requirements](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements), but the short version is this:

1. **Be a real, registered business.** You need a formed company — typically a US C-corp, LLC, or similar entity — with an EIN (Employer Identification Number). Sole proprietors without a formal structure are generally not the target customer.
2. **Have a business bank account with funds in it.** Brex connects to your business banking to verify cash. Funded startups (for example, companies that have raised capital) are the classic fit; revenue-based businesses can qualify based on consistent cash flow. As a rule of thumb from the published guidance, companies holding meaningful cash balances (the site discusses a $50,000 funded-startup cash threshold) present the strongest profile.
3. **Have a physical US business address** (not just a PO box) and basic company documentation handy — formation documents, EIN letter, and ID for the applicant.
4. **Know who will be the account owner.** One founder or finance lead should apply and own the setup; you can add your co-founder and team immediately after approval.

Notice what is not on the list: years in business, profitability, or a founder's personal credit history. A pre-revenue startup with a fresh seed round can be a stronger applicant than a five-year-old business with thin, erratic cash. That inversion is the whole point of a [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) built for startups — though strictly speaking it is a charge card, and that distinction is a feature, not a footnote.

## Day 1: Approval Day — Secure the Account and Set the Skeleton

Approval day feels great for about ten minutes, and then a founder's real job starts: configuration. Resist the urge to immediately order cards for everyone. Day 1 is about the skeleton of the account.

**Morning: lock down access.** Complete your first [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) walkthrough properly — set a strong password, enable two-factor authentication, and confirm the account owner details are correct. Add your co-founder or finance lead as an admin immediately; a finance system with a single point of failure is a risk you do not need during a fundraising trip or a holiday.

**Midday: connect the plumbing.** If you have not already, confirm the linked business bank account is the operating account you actually run the company from, and set your repayment schedule. Daily repayment smooths cash flow and can support higher effective limits; monthly repayment batches everything into one statement cycle. Neither is "correct" — pick the one your bookkeeper will thank you for.

**Afternoon: define the chart of accounts.** Before a single dollar is spent, map your spending categories — software, travel, marketing, meals, equipment — to your accounting categories. If you use an accounting platform, connect it today, while there are zero transactions to reconcile. Future-you, at month-end close, is already grateful.

By the end of Day 1 you have spent nothing, and that is exactly right. You have built the container. Tomorrow you put cards in it.

## Day 2: Issue the Cards — Virtual First, Physical Second

Day 2 is card day, and the order matters. Issue **virtual cards first**. The moment your account is active you can generate virtual [brexcard](https://brexcardadvisor.com) numbers for recurring software and online spend. Move your SaaS stack onto them the same day: the CRM, the cloud bill, the design tools. Every subscription that moves off your personal card is one less reimbursement form and one less personal liability.

Then order **physical cards** for the people who spend in the real world — founders first, then team leads who travel or buy in person. Physical cards take a few days to arrive, which is another reason to start with virtual.

A founder-level tip that saves real pain: create **dedicated virtual cards per vendor** for your biggest subscriptions. When a card is ever compromised or a vendor needs cutting, you replace one number instead of your company's entire purchasing ability. This per-vendor discipline is one of the quiet superpowers of managing a [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) portfolio instead of a single shared card, and it takes ten minutes on Day 2.

## Day 3: Set Limits and Controls Before Habits Form

If Day 2 was about enabling spending, Day 3 is about fencing it — kindly, sensibly, and before anyone develops habits you will have to undo. Your overall [brex card limit](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit) is determined by your business's finances, but within that ceiling, you decide how much any individual card can spend, and on what.

Set three layers of control today:

- **Per-person limits.** A founder's card and an intern's card should not carry the same ceiling. Set monthly limits by role: executives highest, managers mid-range, individual contributors at what their job genuinely requires.
- **Per-card limits for vendors.** That dedicated cloud-services virtual card can carry a high limit because the spend is predictable; the general-purpose team card should not.
- **Category controls.** Where the platform allows category rules, use them. Meals and travel on the road are legitimate; the same categories at odd amounts from a desk-based employee deserve an automatic pause.

Why do this on Day 3 rather than "later, when we're bigger"? Because controls installed before spending starts are culture; controls installed after an incident are punishment. Your team will experience the Day 3 version as "this company is organized." The post-incident version reads as "this company doesn't trust us." Same rules, completely different story.

Also decide today what happens at the edges: who approves an over-limit request, how fast, and through which channel. A one-line policy — "DM the finance lead, approvals within one business day" — beats a perfect policy that lives nowhere.

## Day 4: Roll Out to the Team Without the Chaos

Day 4 is people day. Up to now the account has been a founder project; today it becomes company infrastructure. The rollout for a typical [brex business](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) setup has four moves:

1. **Announce it properly.** One short message to the whole team: the company now has corporate cards, here is who gets one, here is the one-page spending policy, here is what changed (no more personal cards for company spend, no more reimbursement queues).
2. **Issue in waves.** Founders and budget owners first (already done on Day 2), then managers, then everyone else who spends. Waves let you fix small confusions with three people instead of thirty.
3. **Pair every card with the policy.** A card without a policy is an argument waiting to happen. The policy itself can be five bullets: spend only on business needs, keep receipts above a set amount, no personal purchases ever, flag mistakes immediately (honesty is rewarded, silence is not), and ask before anything unusual.
4. **Set the receipt habit on day one of each person's card.** The rule that actually works is "photo of the receipt at the moment of purchase, attached the same day." Modern spend platforms let employees snap and attach receipts as they go. Retrofitting this habit in month three is miserable; installing it on Day 4 is free.

Expect two questions from the team. First: "Does this affect my personal credit?" No — there is no personal guarantee and the card is underwritten on the business, which is the defining trait of the [brex corporate credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) model. Second: "What if I need to spend more than my limit?" Point them at the Day 3 approval path you already defined. You did define it, right?

## Day 5: Wire Up Expense Management and Close the Loop With Accounting

A corporate card that is not connected to your books is just a faster way to create a month-end mess. Day 5 is integration day.

**Connect your accounting software** if you skipped it on Day 1, and verify the category mapping with a real transaction — by now a few software charges have landed, so you can watch them flow into the right accounts. **Turn on receipt matching and transaction memos.** Decide which fields are mandatory: for most startups, receipt + business purpose memo on anything above a small threshold is the sweet spot between control and friction.

**Set approval workflows for the exceptions.** Routine, in-policy spend should flow untouched; that is the point of pre-set limits. Out-of-policy spend, over-limit requests, and out-of-pocket reimbursements (they will still happen occasionally) should route to a named approver. Configure this now, while volume is low and you can still read every line.

**Schedule the weekly review.** Put thirty minutes on the calendar — founder or finance lead, every week, same time — to scan transactions. In the first month this catches misconfigurations (wrong categories, a subscription on the wrong card). Later it becomes your cheapest fraud detector and your clearest window into where the company actually spends money, as opposed to where the budget says it does.

This is the day the [brex business credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) stops being a payment method and becomes a system. Payment methods save minutes. Systems save weekends.

## Day 6: Understand the Money — Rewards, Value, and Fees

By Day 6 the mechanics are running, so take an hour to understand the economics: what you earn, what you pay, and where the real value hides.

**Rewards.** Brex pays points on spending, with elevated earn rates in the categories startups actually use — think rideshare, travel booked through the platform, restaurants, and software — and points can be redeemed for cash back, travel, or statement-style value. The details and current multipliers are laid out in our [brex card rewards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards) guide; the strategic point for founders is simpler. Route spend deliberately. The subscriptions you moved onto virtual cards on Day 2 and the travel your team books are exactly the categories where points accumulate, and at startup spending levels the annual haul is material — often enough to fund a team offsite.

This is where the phrase [brex value](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards) earns its keep: the return is not just points. It is points plus the finance hours not spent on reimbursements, plus the personal liability you removed from your own credit file, plus limits that scale with your company instead of against it.

**Fees.** The fee picture is refreshingly short, and our [brex card fees](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-fees) breakdown covers it in full. The headlines: $0 annual fee for the card itself, no foreign transaction fees on card spend in the usual configuration, and a plan cost of $0 (Essentials) or $12 per user/month (Premium) for the software layer. International and ancillary items can carry their own charges (the site's guidance mentions up to 3% FX markup in specific contexts), so if your team spends heavily abroad, read that page before Day 6 ends. What you will not find: interest charges, because a charge card paid in full has no revolving balance to charge interest on.

**The one economic rule to internalize:** because balances are paid in full on a daily or monthly cycle, Brex is a cash-flow discipline tool, not a financing tool. Founders who treat it as a credit line get a rude surprise; founders who treat it as a smarter debit system with rewards get the full benefit.

## Day 7: Look at the Banking Side — and Retire a Persistent Myth

Finish your first week by looking beyond the card. Founders regularly ask some version of "so is [brex bank](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) where my money sits now?" — usually after seeing the word "bank" in search results or a stray reference to "brexbank" in a forum thread. Let us retire the myth cleanly, because misunderstanding it leads to sloppy decisions.

Brex is not a bank. The card and spend platform sit alongside a business account offering — cash management features delivered through partner banks — and you can read exactly how that account works on our [brex business account](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) page. The practical picture for a founder looks like this: your operating cash can live in a Brex business account, your card limit is informed by the cash Brex can see, and card spend, bill payments, and reimbursements all run through one dashboard instead of four logins. Whether you move your primary operating account or simply connect your existing bank is a treasury decision, not a card decision — plenty of startups connect an external account and never move their cash, and plenty consolidate everything for the simplicity.

Day 7 actions, then:

- **Decide where operating cash lives** for the next quarter, and configure accounts accordingly.
- **Set up bill pay** for the two or three big recurring vendors that do not take cards, so payables stop living in someone's inbox.
- **Invite your bookkeeper or accountant** with view-level access. External accountants consistently rank "client gave us access in week one" among their favorite sentences.
- **Run your first mini-close.** Export the week's transactions, check them against your accounting categories, fix the two mappings that are inevitably wrong. A seven-day month-end rehearsal now prevents a seven-alarm fire later.

Week one is now complete: approved, configured, issued, controlled, rolled out, integrated, understood, and connected. That is the whole playbook — and notice that only about an hour of it was the application itself.

## The First Month: From Setup to System

What separates founders who "have a Brex card" from founders who have a finance system is the first month. Three habits matter most.

**Keep the weekly review sacred.** Fifteen to thirty minutes, transactions scanned, anomalies questioned while memories are fresh. In week two you will find the duplicate subscription and the personal charge someone made by accident. Finding them in week two is a conversation; finding them in month six is an investigation.

**Revisit limits with real data.** Your Day 3 limits were educated guesses. By week four you have actuals. Raise the ceiling that keeps blocking a legitimate team, lower the one nobody touches, and adjust the [brex card limit](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit) allocation between cards accordingly. Limits are a living setting, not a one-time decision.

## Five First-Week Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Issuing everyone a card on Day 1.** Excitement is not a rollout plan. Waves beat floods.
2. **Skipping the per-vendor virtual cards.** One shared number for every subscription means one compromise breaks everything.
3. **Treating the charge card like a credit line.** Balances are paid in full. Among all [brex business credit cards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) and charge cards in this class, the ones that hurt founders are the ones used as financing.
4. **Setting limits after the first incident.** Controls installed in calm weather are culture; installed after a problem, they are blame.
5. **Forgetting the humans.** The rollout message, the five-bullet policy, the named approver — these unglamorous artifacts do more for clean books than any software feature.

## Founder FAQs

**How fast can a startup actually get approved?**
Well-prepared applicants — registered entity, EIN, funded business bank account, matching documents — often move from application to approval quickly, sometimes the same day. Requests for an extra document are the most common source of delay, which is why the readiness checklist comes before the application in this playbook.

**Will applying affect my personal credit?**
No. There is no personal credit check for the card and no personal guarantee; underwriting is based on the business. This is the structural difference between a [brex corporate credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) and a traditional small-business card with the founder's name on the hook.

**What cash position do we need?**
It varies by business model. Funded startups are evaluated largely on cash in the bank — published guidance on our site discusses a $50,000 threshold for funded startups as a reference point — while revenue-generating businesses can be evaluated on cash flow. The requirements page linked earlier in this guide has the current detail.

**Daily or monthly repayment — which should we choose?**
Daily repayment keeps balances near zero, smooths cash flow, and can support stronger limits; monthly repayment gives you one predictable statement cycle. Choose with your bookkeeper, and remember you are choosing an operating rhythm, not passing a test.

**What does the card cost, honestly?**
The card itself: $0 annual fee. The platform: Essentials at $0 or Premium at $12 per user/month, with the fee details — including specific international items — itemized on the fees page linked on Day 6.

**Can my whole team really get cards?**
Yes — issuing cards to employees is the normal operating mode, each with its own limit and controls. That is precisely what people mean when they describe [brex business credit cards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) as team infrastructure rather than a founder perk.

**Is my money held by a bank with Brex?**
Brex is not itself a bank; its business account features are provided through partner banking relationships. Your card limit and account features are driven by the cash position visible to Brex, whether that cash sits in a Brex business account or a connected external account.

**Where do I go when I am stuck in the dashboard?**
Start with the basics — sign out, sign back in through the official [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) page, and confirm you are using the admin email from your application. Most "I can't see my cards" panics are wrong-account problems wearing a trench coat.
