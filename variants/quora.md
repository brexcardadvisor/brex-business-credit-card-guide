## First, What Exactly Is Brex?

[Brex](https://brexcardadvisor.com) is a financial technology company founded in 2017 in San Francisco. The founding insight was simple but powerful: traditional banks judge a business card application almost entirely on the founder's personal credit history, which produces absurd results. A startup could have two million dollars sitting in its bank account from a seed round and still get offered a $3,000 limit — or an outright rejection — because the company itself was only eight months old and the founder's personal credit file was thin.

Brex flipped that. Instead of underwriting the person, it underwrites the business: cash balances, revenue, funding history and spending patterns. That single change is why the [brex corporate card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) became almost a default choice in the startup world, and why the platform later expanded from cards into expense management, business accounts, bill pay, travel and reimbursements.

A bit of recent history worth knowing: the [brex company](https://brexcardadvisor.com) was acquired by Capital One, with the acquisition completed on April 7, 2026. ## How the Brex Card Actually Works (This Is the Part Most People Get Wrong)

People use a lot of different names for this product — the [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card), the [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card), the [brex corporate credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) — but they are all talking about one thing: the Brex Card, which is technically a **business charge card**, not a revolving credit card.

Here is the difference, because it changes how you should use it:

- A revolving credit card lets you carry a balance month to month and charges you interest on whatever you don't pay off.
- A charge card does not revolve. With Brex, the balance is repaid in full on a fixed schedule — either **daily** or **monthly**, depending on how your account qualifies.

There is no purchase APR to compare because you are never meant to borrow over time. In exchange for that discipline, you get three things founders care about a great deal: **no personal guarantee**, **no personal credit check** (Brex states that applying does not affect your personal credit score), and limits based on your company's actual finances rather than your FICO score.

Daily vs. monthly repayment is worth understanding before you apply:

- **Daily payments:** each day's cleared transactions are collected from your account, so the balance stays near zero. Daily-payment limits are closely tied to the funds you hold in a Brex business account, which makes this the natural setup for companies that keep their operating cash inside Brex.
- **Monthly payments:** the full statement balance is due each cycle, more like a traditional card's rhythm — but still in full, every time. This path typically requires a stronger cash position, which is where the roughly $50,000 minimum cash balance for venture-funded startups comes in.

If your business model genuinely depends on carrying balances for three or four months at a time, stop here: no amount of clever setup will make a charge card do a revolving card's job, and you should look at conventional [brex business credit cards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) alternatives instead. I'll come back to that in the "who should NOT use Brex" section.

## Step by Step: How Applying and Setting Up Actually Works

This is the part most reviews skip, so here is the real sequence, in order:

**Step 1: Check your eligibility before you touch the application.**
Confirm your company is incorporated in the U.S., has an EIN, operates in the U.S. and has a physical U.S. street address. Then sanity-check your finances against the path you want: funded startups generally need around $50,000 in cash for monthly payments, while daily payments hinge on the funds you will hold in the Brex business account. If you are a sole proprietor, do not apply — sole proprietors are not eligible, full stop.

**Step 2: Gather your documents in one place.**
You will want your EIN and incorporation details (legal name, state of incorporation, entity type), your physical business address (and a supporting document like a lease, utility bill or bank statement if verification is requested), identification details for beneficial owners and the controlling officer, and either a connection to your business bank account or recent financial statements. Having this ready is the difference between a fast approval and a week of back-and-forth emails.

**Step 3: Start the application and create your admin account.**
On the Brex website, you begin the signup with your work email. This account becomes the one you use at the [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) page every day, so use an email address you will control for the long term — not a contractor's inbox or a temporary alias.

**Step 4: Enter company information carefully.**
Legal business name, EIN, entity type, industry, address and a plain-English description of what the company does. Accuracy matters more than speed here: mismatches between your application, your incorporation records and your bank details are the single most common cause of verification delays.

**Step 5: Complete identity and compliance verification.**
Brex verifies the business and the people behind it — beneficial owners and the controlling officer provide identification details. This is a regulatory Know-Your-Customer check. It is **not** a personal credit pull, and it does not affect anyone's credit score.

**Step 6: Connect your bank or submit financials.**
Brex needs to see company finances to size your limit. Most applicants securely connect their primary business bank account during onboarding. Larger companies, or those seeking monthly payments, may upload financial statements instead — income statement, balance sheet, cash flow. If you are going the daily-payments route, funding the Brex business account happens around this stage, because your limit is primarily driven by the balance held there.

**Step 7: Confirm your repayment schedule.**
Based on the underwriting outcome, your account is set to daily or monthly payments. There is no minimum-payment option either way — the full amount is collected on schedule.

**Step 8: Wait for the underwriting decision.**
Straightforward applications from clearly qualifying companies are usually decided quickly. If Brex asks for additional documentation, send exactly what was requested. Partial or "close enough" answers are the most common self-inflicted delay in the whole process.

**Step 9: Configure the account before issuing a single card.**
This step separates companies that love Brex from companies that merely have Brex. Before cards go out: create spend limits and budgets by team or purpose (software, travel, marketing), set approval policies (who approves what, above which amounts), set receipt and memo rules so documentation is captured at the moment of purchase, invite your finance team with the right roles, and connect your accounting software.

**Step 10: Issue cards and activate them.**
Issue physical cards to people who spend in person and virtual cards for subscriptions, vendors and online purchases. Every card sits under its own spend limit, so no employee ever has access to the company's entire line. The moment those cards are active, your [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card) program is live — and because of Step 9, it is live with controls already in place.

## Eligibility: Who Qualifies, and Who Doesn't

Brex publishes its requirements fairly openly, and you can read the full breakdown on the [requirements page](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements), but the essentials are these.

**Identity and structure requirements:**

- A U.S. Employer Identification Number (EIN)
- U.S. incorporation — C-corps and LLCs are the typical profiles
- U.S. operations and a physical U.S. address (a P.O. box or private mailbox does not count)
- Sole proprietors and unincorporated partnerships are **not eligible**

**Financial requirements (these vary by repayment type):**

- For **daily payments**, qualification can rest on factors like equity investment or revenue, and your working limit is primarily tied to the funds held in your Brex business account.
- For **monthly payments**, Brex looks harder at overall financial strength. Funded startups generally need a minimum cash balance — currently around $50,000, with some exceptions — and larger or commercial companies are assessed on revenue and financial statements.

Two honest caveats. First, crossing a published threshold never guarantees approval; Brex also weighs your business model, source of funds and expected spending patterns. Second, the absence of a personal credit check does not mean "no scrutiny" — it means the scrutiny is aimed at the business, where it belongs.

This is exactly why the [brex corporate card for startups](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-for-startups) has the reputation it does: a freshly funded company with real cash and zero credit history is precisely the profile traditional banks fumble and Brex was built to serve. The flip side is equally true — a pre-incorporation side project, however promising, is not yet a Brex customer.

## How Limits Are Calculated (and Why They Move)

Your Brex limit is not a single fixed number handed down at approval and forgotten. There are really two layers:

1. **The company limit** — the ceiling for total card spending across the business.
2. **Card and spend limits** — the controls you set underneath that ceiling: per-employee limits, per-team budgets, per-purpose caps, temporary travel limits, and so on.

A company might have a very large overall limit while a new hire's card is capped at a modest monthly amount, plus a separate, time-limited travel budget for one conference. That layering is the entire point of the platform.

What determines the company limit? From Brex's own documentation and onboarding flow, the drivers are business-level: connected bank balances, funds held in the Brex business account, revenue, capital raised, and — for larger applicants — financial statements. For daily-payment accounts, the account balance is the dominant factor, so the limit naturally breathes with your cash position. For monthly-payment accounts, overall financials matter more.

The practical consequence: limits tend to grow as your company grows, and they can shrink if your cash position deteriorates. That is rational underwriting, but it surprises founders who expected a static line. ## Fees: What Does Brex Actually Cost?

This is refreshingly simple compared to most business cards:

- **The card itself: $0 annual fee.** There is no annual fee on the underlying card.
- **The software: two main tiers.** Essentials is $0 per user per month — a genuinely usable free entry point. Premium is published at $12 per user per month and unlocks the more advanced controls, policies and features that larger finance teams tend to need.
- **Foreign currency spending:** international use is supported, but when a transaction requires currency conversion, Brex applies an FX markup of up to 3%. If your team spends heavily in foreign currencies — overseas contractors, international ad platforms billing in local currency — model that cost in before you commit.
- **Interest: none, because there is no revolving.** You cannot carry a balance, so there is no purchase APR. The flip side is that there is also no way to spread a large purchase over several months.

Late payment deserves one blunt sentence: because the balance must be paid in full on schedule, treat repayment as a hard operational deadline, the same way you treat payroll. The current, complete picture lives on our [brex card fees](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-fees) page, and it is worth a quick check against the official pricing before you sign up, because plans do evolve.

## Rewards, and What People Actually Mean by "Brex Value"

Yes, there is a points program. Card spending earns a base reward, and Brex's rewards programs offer category multipliers in the kinds of categories businesses actually spend on — rideshare, travel, restaurants, software and the like. Points can typically be directed toward things like travel, statement value or partner transfers, depending on your plan and the current program terms.

But here is the thing I have come to believe after looking at this closely: points are the least interesting part of the economics. When finance teams talk about [brex value](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards), they are usually talking about the total package:

- **Hours returned to the finance team** — no more chasing receipts at month-end, because receipt and memo rules capture documentation at the point of purchase.
- **Money that never leaves** — out-of-policy spending is prevented by limits set in advance, instead of being discovered on a statement three weeks later.
- **Fraud and waste caught early** — every vendor sits on a visible, dedicated virtual card, so duplicate subscriptions and compromised numbers are easy to spot and fix.
- **A limit that reflects reality** — capacity based on your cash position rather than a founder's personal credit file.
- **Business credit building** — Brex reports payment performance to business credit bureaus, so disciplined use helps build your company's credit profile over time.

Add the points on top of that and the math works nicely for most growing companies. Judge the rewards against your actual spending mix using the [brex card rewards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards) breakdown — just don't let a points multiplier distract you from the operational value, which is where the real return is.

## The Business Account, and the "Is Brex a Bank?" Question

Let me settle this one plainly, because the search traffic proves how common the confusion is: people search for [brex bank](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account), and a surprising number type it as one word — brexbank — expecting a bank's homepage. **Brex is not a bank.** It is a financial technology company; the [brex business](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) account is offered through partner arrangements rather than Brex holding a banking charter of its own.

The account is designed to sit at the center of the platform: hold funds, make payments, and feed the card program. Its importance depends on your repayment type. For daily-payment customers it is close to essential, because the limit is primarily driven by the balance held there. For monthly-payment customers it is more of a visibility and convenience layer on top of revenue-based underwriting.

The healthiest way to think about it: Brex is an operating system for company money — cards, account, expenses, bills and travel behind one login — not a wholesale replacement for every banking relationship your company will ever have.

## Pros and Cons, Without the Marketing Gloss

**Pros**

- No personal guarantee, and applying does not affect your personal credit
- Limits based on company finances — often dramatically higher for funded or high-revenue companies than anything a personal-credit-based process would offer
- Physical, virtual and purchasing cards, each with granular spend controls
- Expense management, reimbursements, bill pay and travel integrated in one platform
- $0 annual fee on the card, with a free Essentials plan to start
- Receipt automation and accounting integrations that save real finance-team hours
- On-time payments reported to business credit bureaus

**Cons**

- Charge card only: the balance must be paid in full, daily or monthly — there is no revolving option
- Sole proprietors and unincorporated businesses cannot qualify at all
- Cash and revenue thresholds will exclude many very small or very early businesses
- The FX markup (up to 3%) adds up for heavy foreign-currency spenders
- The advanced controls live behind the paid per-user Premium plan
- For a three-person team with simple needs, the platform can honestly be more than you require

## Who Should NOT Use Brex

I promised this section at the start, and I think it's the most useful part of the answer:

1. **Sole proprietors and unincorporated businesses.** Not a judgment call — you are ineligible.
2. **Businesses that need to revolve balances.** If your cash flow requires spreading purchases over months, a charge card will hurt you. Get a true revolving card.
3. **Very early, pre-funding projects.** Without incorporation, an EIN and either meaningful cash or revenue, approval is not realistic yet. Revisit Brex after the fundraise or the revenue arrives.
4. **Tiny teams with trivial spending.** If one founder puts $800 a month on a card, the controls and automation are solving problems you don't have.
5. **Companies whose spend is overwhelmingly foreign-currency.** That up-to-3% conversion markup deserves a hard look against FX-friendly alternatives.

For everyone else in that excluded list, the honest move is to compare the options in our [brex alternatives](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-alternatives) guide — traditional bank business cards for revolving credit and ecosystems, Ramp as the closest spend-management rival, and banking-first platforms if the account matters more to you than the controls.
