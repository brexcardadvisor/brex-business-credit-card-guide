## The Problem: Your Personal Card Is Not a Finance System

Most online businesses start the same way. The founder opens an ad account, enters a personal card, and the store takes off. Then the cracks appear, usually in this order:

1. **The ad account pauses at the worst moment.** Meta or Google hits the personal card's limit mid-campaign — during your biggest sale weekend — and ads stop until someone notices.
2. **The limit reflects the founder, not the store.** A shop doing $80,000 a month in revenue can still be stuck with a $5,000 card limit because the limit was set by one person's personal credit history.
3. **Renewals become a scavenger hunt.** Twenty tools renew on twenty dates. One expired or replaced personal card number can break every subscription at once.
4. **Bookkeeping turns into archaeology.** At month end, someone scrolls a personal statement trying to remember which charges were business, which supplier, which campaign.
5. **The founder carries the risk personally.** A personal guarantee means business spending problems follow the owner home.

A corporate card program built for businesses solves these problems structurally, not with willpower. That is the category Brex sits in.

Brex is not for every seller, and it is better to know that now than after starting an application. You are a realistic candidate if:

- Your business is **incorporated in the U.S.** (LLC, C-corp or another incorporated entity)
- You have a **U.S. EIN** from the IRS
- Your business has a **physical U.S. address** (not a P.O. box or mailbox service)

## Step by Step: Setting Up Brex for Your Online Business

### Step 1: Confirm your entity and gather documents

Have ready: your EIN, incorporation details, physical U.S. business address, beneficial-owner identification for compliance verification, and access to your main business bank account. If your store still runs through personal accounts, open and fund the business account first — Brex's underwriting reads business finances, and clean inputs produce a cleaner decision.

### Step 2: Start the application

Begin the signup on the Brex website with your work email. This account becomes your daily [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login), so register it with an email your business controls permanently.

### Step 3: Enter company information exactly as recorded

Legal name, EIN, entity type, industry and address must match your incorporation and banking records. Mismatches between the application, the bank and the IRS records are the most common avoidable cause of verification delays.

### Step 4: Complete identity verification

Owners and the controlling officer provide identification details for regulatory Know-Your-Customer checks. This is a compliance step, not a credit check — Brex does not underwrite the [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) using personal credit, and applying does not affect your personal credit score.

### Step 5: Connect your business bank account

Connect the account where store revenue actually lands. This connection is what lets Brex size your limit to your business. Revenue-based and cash-based paths both start here; larger applicants may submit financial statements as well.

### Step 6: Choose daily or monthly payments

Match the rhythm to your cash flow, as discussed above: daily if revenue is steady and lands in the connected account, monthly if payouts arrive in waves. Remember that the full balance is due on whichever schedule you choose.

### Step 7: Set up your card architecture before spending a dollar

This is the step most sellers skip and later wish they had not. Before any purchase, create:

1. **A virtual card per ad platform** (Meta, Google, TikTok, Pinterest) with limits sized to each platform's normal monthly spend plus headroom for sales events.
2. **A virtual card per tool category** — or per tool, if you want maximum visibility.
3. **A purchasing card for inventory suppliers**, separate from marketing spend, so cost of goods never hides inside ad budgets.
4. **Physical employee cards** only for people who genuinely spend in person (trade shows, local supplies), each with a modest limit.
5. **A travel limit** that only activates when someone actually travels to a supplier, expo or warehouse.

### Step 8: Configure budgets, approvals and receipt rules

Attach each card to a budget (Advertising, Software, Inventory, Operations). Set approval thresholds — small renewals flow automatically; a large inventory buy needs a second pair of eyes. Turn on receipt prompts so documentation attaches at purchase time instead of being chased at month end.

### Step 9: Move your ad platforms over, one at a time

Update the billing card in one ad platform, confirm a successful charge, then move to the next. Staggering the migration means a typo in one card number can never pause all your advertising at once. Keep the old card active (but unused) for two billing cycles as a safety net, then close the loop.

### Step 10: Migrate subscriptions and set a monthly review

Move each tool's billing to its new virtual card as renewals approach. Then put a recurring 20-minute monthly review in the calendar: scan per-card spend, freeze cards for tools nobody uses, and raise or lower platform limits ahead of seasonal peaks. The [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card) system does the tracking; the review is where the savings actually happen.

## Paying for Inventory: Using Brex Beyond the Card

Inventory is where e-commerce cash flow gets serious, and Brex's role divides into two parts:

- **Card payments to suppliers.** Where a supplier accepts cards, paying on your inventory purchasing card keeps the purchase inside your budgets and documentation flow. Watch one number, though: the **FX markup of up to 3%** on currency conversions. On a $40,000 overseas inventory order, that markup is real money — compare it against wire costs before choosing the payment rail.
- **Bill Pay for invoice-based suppliers.** Many manufacturers and wholesalers invoice rather than charge cards. Brex Bill Pay lets you upload or forward invoices, route them for approval, and schedule payment by ACH, wire or check from the same system as your cards — so a container deposit and an ad bill live in one ledger view.

Yes — virtual cards are the standard way sellers do this, each with its own limit, freezable and replaceable independently.
