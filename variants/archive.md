## Abstract

This reference document provides a structured, end-to-end account of [brex](https://brexcardadvisor.com) as it operates in 2026: a financial technology platform whose core product is a business charge card underwritten against company finances rather than the personal credit of a founder. The document defines the terminology used in the Brex ecosystem, records the company's history from its 2017 founding in San Francisco through its acquisition by Capital One (completed April 7, 2026), describes the product architecture (cards, business account, expense management, bill pay, and travel), sets out the eligibility framework in full, documents the application procedure as a numbered sequence of steps, and analyses the platform's economics — fees, credit-limit methodology, and the rewards model commonly discussed under the heading of brex value. A comparison framework and a references section close the document. Readers who need a shorter, task-oriented treatment may consult the companion guides listed in the references; this document is the comprehensive record.

## 3. Company History

### 3.1 Founding and the underwriting insight (2017)

Brex was founded in 2017 in San Francisco by Pedro Franceschi and Henrique Dubugras, previously the builders of the Brazilian payments company Pagar.me. The founding insight was an underwriting gap: conventional business-card issuers assessed the *founder's* personal credit file, so a young company holding substantial venture capital could be offered a negligible limit — or declined — because the business itself had no credit history and its founder had a thin one. Brex inverted the assessment. It underwrote the *business*: cash on hand, funding raised, revenue, and observed spending patterns. That inversion is the origin of the [brex corporate card for startups](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-for-startups), the use case that made the company prominent in the technology sector.

### 3.2 Platform expansion

From the card, Brex expanded into adjacent finance functions: expense management with policy enforcement at the point of purchase, a business account for holding and moving funds, bill pay with approval chains, reimbursements, and travel booking. The strategic result is that Brex is more accurately described as finance software with a card attached than as a card with an app attached.

### 3.3 Acquisition by Capital One (2026)

In January 2026, Capital One announced its agreement to acquire Brex; the acquisition was completed on **April 7, 2026**. Brex continues to operate under its own brand within Capital One, with its leadership retained. Readers consulting reviews published before April 2026 should treat ownership references in those documents as superseded. This reference document reflects the post-acquisition position throughout.

## 4. Product Architecture

### 4.1 Card types

All Brex cards draw on a single company limit and are administered in one system:

- **Employee cards** — physical or virtual cards for individual staff spending (travel, meals, supplies), each carrying its own cardholder-level limit.
- **Purchasing cards (P-cards)** — cards dedicated to operational and vendor spend, such as software subscriptions, cloud infrastructure, and advertising platforms. Isolating a vendor on a dedicated purchasing card means a card replacement never disturbs other credentials, and a subscription survives staff changes.
- **Virtual cards** — number-only cards created instantly for a defined purpose, vendor, or ceiling; the preferred instrument for recurring online charges.

### 4.2 Charge-card mechanics

The Brex card is a charge card. Two repayment configurations exist:

- **Daily payments** — cleared transactions are collected from the company's account each day, keeping the outstanding balance near zero.
- **Monthly payments** — the statement balance falls due in full each cycle.

There is no minimum-payment option and no revolving facility. In exchange, there is **no personal guarantee**, and Brex states that applying does not affect the applicant's personal credit score, because underwriting is performed against the business.

### 4.3 The business account

The Brex business account is the funds-holding component of the platform: companies hold balances, make payments, and fund card activity from it. Its architectural significance is greatest for daily-payment customers, whose card limits are tied primarily to the funds held in the account. For monthly-payment customers, underwritten more heavily on revenue and financial statements, the account functions principally as a consolidation and visibility layer. Because Brex is a fintech and not a bank, account services are delivered through partner institutions; the recurring user confusion catalogued in the definitions section (brex bank, brexbank) should be read with that fact in mind.

### 4.4 Expense management, bill pay, and travel

- **Policies before purchases.** Spend limits and rules are configured in advance, so non-compliant purchases can be prevented rather than merely reported.
- **Automated documentation.** Receipts are captured and matched to transactions; memo prompts occur at the time of purchase; categorisation is AI-assisted.
- **Approvals and exceptions.** Out-of-policy items route to reviewers in the system.
- **Reimbursements.** Out-of-pocket employee expenses are submitted, approved, and repaid in the same environment as card spend.
- **Bill Pay.** Invoices are uploaded or forwarded, data is extracted, approvals are chained, and payment is scheduled by ACH, check, wire, or card.
- **Travel.** Bookings are made within the platform and bound to travel budgets.
- **Accounting integration.** Coded transactions synchronise to supported accounting platforms, shortening the month-end close. Brex also reports payment performance to business credit bureaus, so disciplined use can contribute to the company's business credit profile.

## 5. Eligibility Framework

Eligibility has two layers: identity requirements, which are absolute, and financial requirements, which vary by repayment configuration. The complete reference treatment is maintained at [brex card requirements](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements); the framework is summarised here.

### 5.1 Business identity requirements

An applicant company must have:

1. A U.S. Employer Identification Number (EIN) issued by the IRS.
2. Valid U.S. incorporation (for example, a C-corporation or LLC).
3. U.S. operations and a physical U.S. street address; a P.O. box or private mailbox does not satisfy this condition.
4. An incorporated form. **Sole proprietors and unincorporated partnerships are not eligible**, and no financial profile cures that defect.

### 5.2 Financial requirements

- **Daily payments.** Qualification may rest on equity investment, on revenue above Brex's published thresholds (one common published path cites annual revenue above $500,000), or on funds held.
- **Monthly payments.** Funded startups generally require a minimum cash balance — currently published at approximately **$50,000** for venture-funded startups, with some exceptions — while established and larger companies are assessed on revenue and financial statements.

### 5.3 Assessment caveats

Meeting a published threshold is a necessary, not a sufficient, condition. Brex additionally reviews the business model, source of funds, financial health, and expected spending patterns. Conversely, two common assumptions are false: Brex does **not** underwrite using the founder's personal credit score, and it does **not** require a personal guarantee.

## 6. Application Procedure

The following numbered procedure records the application and onboarding sequence as documented for the 2026 edition. Steps should be executed in order.

**Step 1 — Perform an eligibility self-assessment.**
Test the company against Section 5 before beginning. Confirm incorporation, EIN, a physical U.S. address, and the cash or revenue profile matching the desired repayment configuration. A sole proprietor should stop at this step and consult the alternatives framework in Section 9.

**Step 2 — Assemble the documentation set.**
Prepare, in one place: the EIN and incorporation particulars (legal name, state of incorporation); evidence of the physical U.S. business address (utility bill, lease, or bank statement where verification is requested); identification particulars for beneficial owners and the controlling officer; recent bank statements or the means to connect the primary business bank account; and, as applicable, funding particulars (amount raised, investors) or financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash-flow statement).

**Step 3 — Initiate the application and create the administrator account.**
Begin the signup flow on the Brex website and register the administrator with a long-term work email and a secure password. These credentials become the account's working [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login); a temporary address should not be used.

**Step 4 — Record the company's particulars.**
Enter the legal business name, EIN, incorporation type and state, industry, business address, and a description of the business. All particulars must match the incorporation records and banking information exactly; transcription mismatches are the most frequent cause of verification delay.

**Step 5 — Complete identity and compliance verification.**
Beneficial owners and the controlling officer provide identification details for the regulatory Know-Your-Customer review. This is a compliance procedure, not a personal credit enquiry, and does not affect personal credit scores.

**Step 6 — Connect financial data sources.**
Connect the primary business bank account through the onboarding flow, or upload financial statements where that route applies. For daily-payment configurations, funding the Brex business account occurs at this stage, because the limit is derived principally from held funds.

**Step 7 — Confirm the repayment configuration.**
The account is set to daily or monthly payments according to the qualification outcome. Record the configuration in the company's finance procedures; in both cases the balance is payable in full, and there is no minimum-payment facility.

**Step 8 — Await the underwriting determination.**
Brex evaluates the application against its criteria. Complete, clearly qualifying applications are typically determined quickly; incomplete documentation extends the timeline. Where further information is requested, supply precisely what was requested.

**Step 9 — Configure controls before issuing any card.**
In order: (1) create spend limits and budgets by team, department, or purpose; (2) set approval policies, including approvers and amount thresholds; (3) set receipt and memo rules; (4) invite the finance team and assign administrator, bookkeeper, and employee roles; (5) connect the accounting platform and confirm the chart-of-accounts mapping.

**Step 10 — Issue and activate cards.**
Issue physical cards to employees who spend in person and virtual cards for online purchases, subscriptions, and vendors, each bound to its spend limit. Cardholders activate their cards and, where supported, add virtual cards to mobile wallets. The [brex corporate card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) program is then operational.

## 7. Account Access and the Dashboard

### 7.1 Sign-in

Day-to-day administration begins at the [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login): administrators and employees authenticate with the credentials created at onboarding, with two-factor authentication and single sign-on available according to plan. The companion reference on access, mobile sign-in, and the resolution of common sign-in failures is the [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) guide.

### 7.2 Administrative view

The dashboard is organised around control rather than retrospective reporting:

- **Cards** — every physical, virtual, and purchasing card, its limit, and recent activity; cards may be frozen or replaced from this view.
- **Expenses** — transactions in near-real time, with receipts and memos attached and exceptions flagged.
- **Budgets and spend limits** — utilisation against each budget, with in-place adjustment.
- **Payments and bills** — scheduled payments, Bill Pay invoices, and upcoming card repayments.
- **Reports** — export and synchronisation of coded transactions to the accounting system.

### 7.3 Cardholder view

Employees see a reduced interface: their own cards, their remaining limit, and receipt capture. The asymmetry is deliberate — compliance effort is minimised for the spender and consolidated for the overseer.

## 8. Economics

### 8.1 Fees and plan pricing

The card itself carries a **$0 annual fee**. The economic decision therefore concerns the software tiers:

| Item | Published position (2026) |
|---|---|
| Card annual fee | $0 |
| Essentials plan | $0 per user per month |
| Premium plan | $12 per user per month |
| Purchase APR | None (charge card; no revolving) |
| Foreign-currency conversion | FX markup up to 3% where conversion is required |

The Premium tier unlocks the more advanced controls and features; the appropriate tier depends on the complexity of the company's approval chains, policy structure, and international footprint. Because plans evolve, current pricing should be confirmed against the official schedule before commitment; the maintained reference is [brex card fees](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-fees).

Two structural cost observations follow. First, the absence of interest is a consequence of the absence of revolving: a company that needs to spread a purchase over several months cannot do so on this instrument. Second, the obligation to pay in full makes the repayment schedule a hard operational deadline; late payment is the principal financial risk the product creates for its user.

### 8.2 Credit-limit methodology

Two limit layers exist. The *company limit* is the ceiling on aggregate card spending; *cardholder and spend limits* ration that ceiling among persons and purposes. A company may therefore hold a large aggregate limit while an individual salesperson's card remains modest, supplemented by a temporary travel limit for a defined trip.

The company limit is computed from business-level inputs documented by Brex: connected bank balances, funds held in the Brex business account, revenue, capital raised, and financial statements. The weighting differs by configuration — daily-payment limits track held funds closely, while monthly-payment limits rest more on overall financials. Limits are not static: they can rise as the financial profile grows and contract if the cash position deteriorates. The detailed reference is [brex card limit](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit).

### 8.3 Rewards and the brex value calculation

Card spending earns points under Brex's rewards programs, with category multipliers available for common business categories such as rideshare, travel, restaurants, and software, and redemption routes that include travel, statement value, and partner transfers, subject to plan and program terms. The maintained breakdown is [brex card rewards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards).

A points-only assessment understates the case. In the usage of experienced finance teams, [brex value](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards) denotes the aggregate: points earned; receipt-chasing and month-end coding hours eliminated; out-of-policy expenditure prevented ex ante by spend limits; duplicate subscriptions and fraud surfaced early because each vendor sits on a visible, dedicated card; and a spending ceiling that reflects the company's actual cash position. Points are a component of value, not a synonym for it.

## 9. Comparison Framework

Assessment against alternatives should proceed by the problem being solved:

- **Traditional bank business cards** suit companies requiring revolving credit, willing to provide a personal guarantee, or concentrated in a single airline or hotel ecosystem. They offer limited native spend control.
- **Ramp** is the closest structural rival: a corporate-card-plus-software model with its own controls and savings tooling.
- **Divvy and comparable spend platforms** compete on budgeting and controls for small and mid-sized teams.
- **Mercury and banking-first platforms** lead with the account and attach cards to a banking stack — the closest match to what users searching for a brex bank alternative generally intend.

The category-by-category treatment is maintained at [brex alternatives](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-alternatives), and the consolidated evaluative verdict at [brex card review](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-review). In summary form: for incorporated, growing companies able to pay in full and seeking control over team spending, Brex is among the strongest instruments available in 2026; for sole proprietors, balance-carriers, and the smallest businesses, it is the wrong instrument, and no configuration of it cures that mismatch.

## 10. Advantages and Limitations — Summary of Record

**Advantages**

1. No personal guarantee; application does not affect personal credit.
2. Limits derived from company finances, frequently far exceeding personal-credit-based limits for funded or high-revenue companies.
3. Physical, virtual, and purchasing cards under granular, pre-set controls.
4. Cards, expenses, reimbursements, bill pay, and travel in a single platform.
5. $0 card annual fee and a free entry software tier.
6. Receipt automation, AI-assisted categorisation, and accounting integrations with measurable time savings.
7. Payment performance reported to business credit bureaus.

**Limitations**

1. Charge card only: balances are payable in full; no revolving credit exists.
2. Sole proprietors and unincorporated businesses are ineligible.
3. Cash and revenue thresholds exclude many very small or pre-revenue companies.
4. The FX markup (up to 3%) is material for heavy foreign-currency spenders.
5. Advanced software capability is reserved to the paid per-user tier.
6. Platform depth may exceed the requirements of a very small team.
