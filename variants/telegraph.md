## Part 1: The Eligibility Self-Test

Brex is built for incorporated, operating businesses. Before anything else, run through these six checks honestly.

**Check 1 — Your business is incorporated in the United States.**
Brex requires a U.S. Employer Identification Number (EIN) and valid U.S. incorporation — a C-corp, an LLC or another incorporated entity. If your business is not yet incorporated, that is your first task, and it comes before any card application.

**Check 2 — You are not a sole proprietor.**
This is the most common hard stop. Sole proprietors and unincorporated partnerships are not currently eligible for Brex, no matter how healthy the business is. If that is your situation, skip to the alternatives section at the end of this guide — a [brex business credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) alternative built for sole proprietors will serve you better than a declined application.

**Check 3 — You have a physical U.S. business address.**
Brex requires U.S. operations and a real, physical U.S. address. A P.O. box or a private mailbox service address does not count. If your team is fully remote, use the address on your incorporation and banking records — consistency matters more than the address itself, as Part 2 explains.

**Check 4 — Your business is actually operating or funded.**
Brex underwrites real businesses. A funded startup with cash in the bank qualifies on its funding; a revenue-generating company qualifies on its revenue; a pre-revenue, pre-funding project generally does not qualify yet. The [brex corporate card for startups](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-for-startups) path exists precisely because Brex looks at cash and investment, not just trading history.

**Check 5 — You can pay in full, on schedule.**
The Brex Card is a charge card, not a revolving credit card. There is no option to carry a balance and pay a minimum each month: the full amount is collected either daily or monthly, depending on how your account qualifies. If your cash flow depends on spreading card purchases over several months, this product is the wrong shape for you — read Part 3 carefully.

**Check 6 — You do not need a personal credit check — because there isn't one.**
Founders sometimes delay applying because they are worried about their personal credit score. With Brex, that worry does not apply: there is no personal guarantee, and Brex states that applying does not affect your personal credit score. The flip side is that a great personal score will not rescue a business that fails Checks 1 to 5. The business is the applicant.

If you passed all six checks, continue to Part 2. The full requirements breakdown lives on our [requirements page](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements) if you want the fine print on any item.

## Part 2: The Documents Checklist — Assemble This Folder First

Create a folder on your computer called "Brex Application" and do not start the application until every item below is inside it. This single habit prevents most mid-application stalls.

**Identity and incorporation documents**
- [ ] EIN confirmation details — the exact number issued by the IRS
- [ ] Legal company name, exactly as it appears on your incorporation records
- [ ] State of incorporation and incorporation type (C-corp, LLC, etc.)
- [ ] Industry and a one-paragraph description of what your company does

**Address evidence**
- [ ] Physical U.S. business address, matching your bank and incorporation records
- [ ] One supporting document if Brex asks to verify the address — a utility bill, a lease, or a bank statement showing the address

**People documents (for compliance verification)**
- [ ] Full names and dates of birth of all beneficial owners
- [ ] Identification details for beneficial owners and the controlling officer

Brex verifies the business and the people behind it as a regulatory Know-Your-Customer check. This is not a credit check and it does not touch anyone's personal credit score — it is the same identity verification any regulated financial platform performs.

**Financial documents**
- [ ] Login access to your primary business bank account (most applicants connect it securely during onboarding)
- [ ] Recent bank statements, in case a manual review is requested
- [ ] For startups: funding summary — how much you have raised and from whom
- [ ] For revenue-based applicants: recent financial statements — income statement, balance sheet and cash-flow statement

One warning that saves real pain: make sure the company name, address and EIN match **exactly** across every document. A legal name on the incorporation certificate that differs by one word from the name on the bank account is the single most common cause of verification delays, and it is entirely avoidable at this stage.

## Part 3: The Financial Thresholds Checklist

Brex offers two repayment schedules, and which one you qualify for depends on your financial profile. Understanding both before you apply tells you which documents in Part 2 matter most.

**Path A — Daily payments.**
With daily payments, the balance for cleared transactions is collected from your account each day, keeping what you owe near zero. Qualification can be based on factors such as equity investment, or on annual revenue above Brex's published thresholds — currently above $500,000 in annual revenue for one common path. Daily-payment limits are tied closely to the funds you hold in a Brex business account, so if this is your path, plan to fund that account as part of setup.

**Path B — Monthly payments.**
With monthly payments, the full statement balance is due once per cycle. Funded startups generally need a minimum cash balance for this path — Brex currently publishes $50,000 for venture-funded startups, with some exceptions. Commercial and larger companies are assessed on revenue and financial statements instead.

Whichever path you land on, two things are always true. First, meeting a published threshold never guarantees approval — Brex also weighs your business model, source of funds, financial health and expected spending patterns. Second, the [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card) itself carries a $0 annual fee either way; the cost conversation is about software plans, which Part 5 covers.

If neither path fits today, that is useful information, not a dead end. Grow the cash balance or the revenue, or start with one of the alternatives in the final section and revisit Brex later.

## Part 4: The Application, Step by Step

Folder assembled, thresholds understood — now the application itself. It follows the same logic as the checklist: identity first, finances second, configuration last.

**Step 1: Create your admin account.**
Go to the Brex website and start the signup flow. Register with your work email and a strong password. Choose carefully: this becomes the account behind your everyday [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login), so use an email address you will still control in three years, not a contractor's inbox or a temporary alias.

**Step 2: Enter the company information.**
Type the legal business name, EIN, incorporation type and state, industry, business address and short description — copied character-for-character from the documents in your Part 2 folder. Do not improvise a "nicer" version of the company name here.

**Step 3: Complete identity and compliance verification.**
Provide the beneficial-owner and controlling-officer details from your checklist. Have the identification documents open in front of you; entering details from memory is how transposed digits happen.

**Step 4: Connect your bank or submit your financials.**
Most applicants connect their primary business bank account securely at this stage so Brex can see the cash position that drives underwriting. If you are applying on the revenue path, upload the financial statements from your folder instead — or as well.

**Step 5: Confirm your repayment schedule.**
Based on your profile, your account is set to daily or monthly payments. Read which one you have been assigned before accepting. Remember what Part 1 established: this is a charge card, and the full amount must be paid on that schedule. There is no minimum-payment fallback.

**Step 6: Wait for the underwriting decision — and answer requests completely.**
Straightforward applications from clearly qualifying companies are usually decided quickly. If Brex requests more information, send exactly what was asked for, in full. A partial response is the most common self-inflicted delay at this stage; a complete one usually unblocks the review in a single round.

**Step 7: Resist issuing cards immediately.**
Approval is not the finish line — configuration is. Do the Part 5 setup checklist first, while the account is still empty and every decision is easy to change.

## Part 5: The First-Week Setup Checklist

A [brex corporate credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) program succeeds or fails in its first week, long before the first statement. Work through this list in order.

**Day 1 — Structure before cards**
- [ ] Create spend limits for each team, department or purpose (software, travel, marketing, operations)
- [ ] Set approval policies: who approves what, and above which amounts a second approver is required
- [ ] Set receipt and memo rules so documentation is captured at the moment of purchase, not chased at month-end
- [ ] Invite your finance team and assign roles — admin, bookkeeper, employee
- [ ] Connect your accounting software so transactions sync against the right chart of accounts

**Day 2 — Issue the right cards**
Brex issues three kinds of cards, and using the right one for each job is where control comes from:
- **Employee cards** — physical or virtual cards for a person's everyday spending: travel, meals, supplies. Each carries its own cardholder limit inside the relevant spend limit.
- **Purchasing cards** — for vendor and operational spending such as SaaS subscriptions, cloud services and advertising. A vendor on a dedicated purchasing card keeps billing stable when staff change, and a compromised card can be replaced without disturbing anyone else.
- **Virtual cards** — created instantly for one purpose, one subscription or one limit. Ideal for isolating recurring charges so a surprise price increase never touches your main line.

- [ ] Issue physical cards to people who spend in person
- [ ] Create virtual cards for every existing subscription, one per vendor
- [ ] Have each cardholder activate their card and add virtual cards to a mobile wallet where supported

**Day 3 — Understand your limits before you need them**
Your company limit is the ceiling for total card spending; card and spend limits divide that ceiling up. Brex calculates the company limit from business factors: connected bank balances, funds in the business account, revenue, capital raised and financial statements — and it can move as those figures move. Our guide to the [brex card limit](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit) explains the mechanics in depth. Check your limit on day three, while there is still time to plan around it, not on the day a large payment is due.

**Day 4 — Learn the dashboard through the login you will use daily**
Everything after setup runs through the [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) page — our dedicated guide covers two-factor authentication, single sign-on, mobile access and fixes for common sign-in problems. Inside, admins get the control view: every card and its recent activity, expenses arriving in real time with receipts attached, budget consumption, scheduled bill payments and upcoming card repayments, plus reporting and accounting sync. Employees get a deliberately simpler view: their own cards, their remaining limit, and a fast way to photograph a receipt. Spend ten minutes clicking through both views on day four; it is cheaper than learning during a month-end crunch.

**Day 5 — Put the business account to work**
Because so many people search for a [brex bank](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account), one fact deserves repeating in the setup week: Brex is a fintech company, not a bank, and its account services are provided through partner arrangements. The [brex business](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) account still sits at the centre of the platform — you hold funds there, pay from there, and, if you are on daily payments, your limit is driven primarily by the funds held there. Fund it deliberately, not as an afterthought.

## Know the Costs Before the First Statement

The card itself is simple: $0 annual fee. The real pricing decision is the software plan. Brex offers a free entry tier — Essentials at $0 per user per month — and a Premium tier published at $12 per user per month with more advanced controls. Small teams often start on Essentials; complex approval chains and global footprints push teams to Premium. The complete picture is on our [brex card fees](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-fees) page, and you should always confirm current pricing on the official site, because plans evolve.

Two more cost items belong on every pre-application checklist. First, international spending: when a transaction needs a currency conversion, Brex applies an FX markup of up to 3%, so heavy foreign-currency spenders should budget for it. Second, the hidden cost of *not* paying in full: there is no interest, because there is no revolving — but repayment is mandatory, so treat the schedule as a hard operational deadline.

## Five Mistakes This Checklist Exists to Prevent

1. **Applying as a sole proprietor.** It is an automatic dead end — incorporate first, or choose an alternative product.
2. **Inconsistent details.** A company name, address or EIN that differs between documents is the top cause of verification delays.
3. **Starting the application without the folder.** Every mid-application document hunt risks a timeout, a mistake, or an abandoned application.
4. **Issuing cards before setting limits.** Handing out a [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) before budgets and approval policies exist gives every employee access to the whole company line.
5. **Comparing charge cards with revolving cards.** Judging the [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) against a card you intend to carry a balance on is a category error — the products solve different problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**I failed one item on the self-test. Should I apply anyway?**
For Checks 1, 2 and 3 — incorporation, not being a sole proprietor, and a physical U.S. address — no. Those are structural requirements, not judgement calls. Fix the underlying item first.

**Does applying affect my personal credit score?**
No. There is no personal guarantee and no personal-credit underwriting. The verification in Step 3 is an identity and compliance check on the business and its owners.

**How much cash does a startup need before applying?**
For monthly payments, Brex currently publishes a $50,000 minimum cash balance for venture-funded startups, with some exceptions. Daily payments follow a different profile, tied to account funds, equity investment or revenue above published thresholds.

**What exactly should be in my documents folder?**
EIN details, incorporation records, a physical-address document, beneficial-owner and controlling-officer identification, bank access or statements, and either a funding summary (startups) or financial statements (revenue-based applicants). Part 2 lists every item.

**Is Brex a bank?**
No — despite the popular "brexbank" search, Brex is a financial technology company, not a bank. Its business account works through partner arrangements, which is worth understanding before you move operating funds into it.

**Which login will I use after approval?**
The same admin account you create in Step 1, via the standard login page or the mobile app. Enable two-factor authentication for every user during the first-week setup.
