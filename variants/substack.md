## First, a thirty-second refresher on what Brex actually is

Brex started in 2017 in San Francisco, when its founders got frustrated that venture-backed startups — companies with millions in the bank — could not get a simple corporate card without a founder personally guaranteeing the debt. The [brex company](https://brexcardadvisor.com) story since then is one of steady expansion: from a single charge card into cards, expense management, bill pay, travel booking and business accounts, and most recently into a new corporate home, with Capital One's acquisition of Brex completing in April 2026.

The product at the center of it all is still the card. A [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card) is a corporate charge card, not a revolving credit card. There is no personal guarantee, no conventional purchase APR, and the balance is repaid in full — either daily or monthly, depending on the product attached to your account. Limits float with your company's cash position rather than a fixed credit line decided once a year. If you are new to the platform entirely, most people start by typing [brex com](https://brexcardadvisor.com) into a browser and landing on the main site; what matters for this article is what happens after approval, when the points start accumulating.

Everything in the Brex ecosystem — cards, spend limits, approvals, travel, bill pay — runs through one dashboard, and your rewards live there too. Understanding the rewards program means understanding that dashboard, which is why the earning steps below begin with a [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) and end with you knowing exactly which number on the screen to trust.

## How Brex points are earned: the multiplier table, minus the gloss

Brex's standard accelerated program is called Brex Exclusive, and most customers are enrolled in it unless their contract says otherwise. Under the standard monthly-payment version of the program, the published earning schedule looks like this:

- **7x points** on rideshare (Uber, Lyft, taxis)
- **4x points** on flights and hotels booked through Brex Travel
- **3x points** on restaurants
- **2x points** on recurring software subscriptions
- **1x point** on everything else that qualifies

Three features of this schedule deserve more attention than they usually get. First, the multipliers are **uncapped** — there is no annual ceiling after which your [brex business credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) drops to a base rate, which is a genuine advantage over capped category cards. Second, points **do not expire** while the account stays active, so a slow quarter does not torch a balance you built over a year. Third, both physical and virtual cards earn on eligible transactions, which matters when half your SaaS stack lives on virtual card numbers.

Now the caveats, because they are where value leaks. Multipliers are driven by merchant category codes, not by what you actually bought. A team dinner ordered through a delivery platform may not code as a restaurant. A "software" purchase from a general marketplace may code as retail and earn 1x. Travel only earns 4x when it is booked inside Brex Travel as an eligible prepaid booking; the same flight bought on an airline's website earns the base rate. Purchases processed as international transactions generally do not earn points at all. And money movement — ACH payments, wires, checks — never earns, because rewards attach to card spend only.

There is also more than one schedule. Daily-payment customers in certain industries sit on specialized programs: the Software program, for example, currently publishes 8x rideshare, 5x Brex Travel, 4x restaurants and 3x recurring software, while the Advertising program pays 1.5x on ad spend — a category that can dwarf everything else for a marketing-heavy company. Larger organizations (typically 50+ users with committed spend) can negotiate custom uncapped flat-rate rebates instead. The lesson: "Brex earns 7x" is a sentence about one category on one product. Your contract is the final authority on your rates.

## Step by step: earning points without leaving value on the table

Earning well on Brex is less about spending more and more about routing the spending you already have through the right rails. Here is the sequence we recommend to finance leads in the first month:

**Step 1: Confirm your program after sign-in.** Complete your [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) and open the Rewards dashboard. Verify whether you are on standard monthly Brex Exclusive or an industry schedule, and note your exact multipliers. Every decision below depends on this screen, not on a blog post — including this one.

**Step 2: Move travel booking inside Brex Travel.** Flights and prepaid hotels booked in the platform earn 4x instead of 1x, and the points are granted after the trip completes. Make "book it in Brex Travel" a line in your travel policy, not a suggestion.

**Step 3: Put every recurring SaaS subscription on a Brex card.** Recurring software earns 2x on the monthly program (3x on the Software daily program). A dedicated virtual card per vendor also makes cancellations painless later — the rewards are a bonus on top of good hygiene.

**Step 4: Give field teams physical cards for rideshare and meals.** Rideshare at 7x and restaurants at 3x are the two highest everyday categories, and they only trigger if the spend actually runs on the card rather than being reimbursed from a personal card.

**Step 5: Audit merchant coding monthly.** Once a month, scan the transactions feed for big purchases that earned 1x but should have earned more. Miscoded merchants are the silent tax on every category program, and catching one recurring vendor can be worth more than a quarter of optimization elsewhere.

**Step 6: Keep money movement off your rewards expectations.** Bill pay and wires through Brex are operationally useful — we cover them in the perks section — but they earn nothing. Budget your expected points from card spend only, or your forecast will always disappoint you.

Follow those six steps and a [brex corporate card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) program mostly runs itself: the travel books itself into the 4x lane, the software renews into the 2x lane, and the team's ground transport quietly stacks up at 7x.

## The part nobody markets: what one Brex point is actually worth

Here is the single most important fact in this entire article: **a Brex point does not have one value.** It has at least three, and the gap between the best and worst is not a rounding error — it is the difference between a strong program and a mediocre one.

**Brex Travel redemptions: 1 point = $0.01.** When you spend points on eligible prepaid flights and hotels inside Brex Travel, each point is worth a full cent. This is the benchmark lane, the one every multiplier fantasy is quietly based on, and usually the best predictable value in the program.

**Cash and statement credit: 1 point = $0.006.** Redeem the same points as cash into your business account, or as a statement credit against the card balance, and each point is worth six-tenths of a cent. That is a 40% haircut for choosing the flexible option. A statement-credit redemption also cannot be undone once it completes, so it deserves a deliberate click, not a casual one.

**Airline transfers: 1,500 points = 1,000 miles, typically.** Transfers escape the fixed-price world entirely. Whether this lane beats the 1-cent travel benchmark depends on what a mile buys you in the receiving program: if you redeem transferred miles for roughly 2 cents of flight value each, your Brex points were effectively worth about 1.33 cents apiece; if you redeem them poorly, at 1 cent per mile, the same points were worth only about 0.67 cents. Transfers are a skill-based redemption — powerful for travel-savvy teams, a trap for everyone else.

Do the simple multiplication for any balance to see the spread. At 100,000 points, you are holding $1,000 of Brex Travel value, $600 of cash value, or roughly 66,700 airline miles of variable value. Same points, three different wallets. When you evaluate the real [brex value](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards) of the program for your company, the only honest approach is to price your points in the lane you will actually use — and for most companies that never book travel through a portal, that lane is the 0.6-cent one, which changes the entire comparison against cashback.

## Step by step: redeeming points the smart way

Redemption is where disciplined teams separate themselves. The order of operations below is deliberate — it walks from the highest-value lane to the lowest, so you only fall through to a weaker option when the stronger one genuinely does not fit.

**Step 1: Check the live balance and rates in the dashboard.** After sign-in, the Rewards dashboard shows your balance and the current redemption rates for your account. Rates on gift cards and partner offers vary and are displayed live, so treat the dashboard as the price list and everything else (including this newsletter) as commentary.

**Step 2: Price your next trip in Brex Travel first.** If the company has any travel coming up — a conference, a sales trip, an offsite — check whether the flights and prepaid hotels you need are available for points in Brex Travel at the 1-cent rate. Note the fine print: some inventory is excluded (certain low-cost carriers and non-USD or postpaid hotel rates), and taxes or property fees on a hotel stay may still need to be paid by card. Points cover a lot of a trip, not always all of it.

**Step 3: Use points for travel before cash, even if it feels less "real."** Founders often prefer cash because cash is legible. But $1,000 of travel you were going to buy anyway is worth exactly $1,000 to your runway, while the same points converted to cash are worth $600. If the trip is real, the travel lane wins.

**Step 4: Consider airline transfers only with a specific award in mind.** Transfer when you have already found the flight you want and know its cash price, so you can compute the cents-per-mile you are actually getting. Remember that travel-multiplier points post after the trip completes, and refunded award bookings can take a week or more to return points to your balance — do not plan a same-day rebooking on refunded points.

**Step 5: Treat cash and statement credit as the pressure valve, not the plan.** Redeeming at 0.6 cents is not a mistake when the alternative is points sitting idle while cash is tight — runway beats optimization. Just make the choice with open eyes: you are paying a 40% convenience fee for liquidity, and a completed statement-credit redemption cannot be reversed.

**Step 6: Never let points be the reason for a purchase.** Points do not expire while the account is active, so there is no deadline forcing your hand. The correct redemption trigger is "we were going to spend this money anyway," full stop.

## The worked example: one startup, one month of spend

Enough theory. Let us run a complete month through the machine. **The following is an illustrative example with general, rounded numbers — not a quote, not a promise, and not any real company's data.** Imagine a 25-person startup, on the standard monthly-payment Brex Exclusive schedule, that spends $40,000 in a typical month, distributed like this:

| Category | Monthly spend | Multiplier | Points earned |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Rideshare and taxis | $800 | 7x | 5,600 |
| Flights and hotels via Brex Travel | $4,000 | 4x | 16,000 |
| Restaurants and team meals | $1,500 | 3x | 4,500 |
| Recurring software | $6,000 | 2x | 12,000 |
| Everything else | $27,700 | 1x | 27,700 |
| **Total** | **$40,000** | — | **65,800** |

Three observations jump out of this table, and they generalize to almost every company we have ever modeled.

First, the blended earn rate is **1.645 points per dollar** — a long way from 7x, because 69% of the spending sits in the 1x bucket. This is normal. Payroll-adjacent costs, contractors, inventory, and most vendor payments simply do not live in bonus categories, no matter which [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) or competitor you choose.

Second, the redemption lane decides the outcome. Those 65,800 monthly points are worth **$658 in Brex Travel** (an effective 1.65% return on spend), **$394.80 as cash** (0.99%), or about **43,900 airline miles** of it-depends value. Annualized, that is roughly $7,900 of travel value versus $4,740 of cash value from the same spending.

Third, compare against the dull alternative. A flat 2% cashback card on the same $40,000 returns **$800 in cash, every month, no lanes, no portals, no thinking** — $9,600 a year. A flat 1.5% card returns $600. So in this example, Brex used perfectly for travel lands between the two cashback cards, Brex redeemed for cash loses to both, and only Brex-with-skilled-airline-transfers has a realistic path to beating the 2% card — because the transfer lane is the only one that can push effective value above 1 cent per point.

That is the honest shape of the program: a [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) alternative whose ceiling is set by your travel habits and whose floor is set by the 0.6-cent cash rate. Your company's distribution will differ from this example — a travel-heavy sales org will look better, a remote-first team with one giant cloud bill will look worse — which is exactly why the decision framework later in this issue uses your numbers, not ours.

## The perks beyond points: travel, bill pay and the essentials

Points get the headlines, but a meaningful slice of the value in [brex business](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) accounts never touches the rewards ledger at all. Three perks deserve a permanent place in your evaluation.

**Brex Travel as an operating tool, not just a redemption lane.** Booking inside the platform is what unlocks the 4x earning rate, but the operational value is separate: trips are tied to spend limits and policies before money moves, points can pay at checkout, and on the Essentials plan Brex does not charge a trip fee. (Premium and Enterprise travel can carry a per-trip fee depending on contract — one of several quiet differences between plans.) If your team travels monthly, consolidating booking into the same system that issued the cards removes an entire category of receipt-chasing.

**Bill pay that behaves like part of the platform.** Brex's bill pay lets you schedule and pay vendor invoices by ACH or wire from the same dashboard as your cards, with approvals attached. The catch, repeated from earlier: payments earn no points. The value here is control and time — one queue for card spend and invoice spend, one approval chain — not rewards. Teams sometimes describe the combined cash account informally as their [brex bank](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account); more precisely, it is a business account with account and routing numbers for receiving payments, sending ACH and wires, and holding the cash your card limits are based on.

**The Essentials tier costs nothing to stand on.** The card carries no separate annual fee, and the Essentials software plan is $0 per user per month, including cards, expense tooling, accounting integrations, bill pay and reimbursements. Premium, at $12 per user per month, buys advanced policies, dynamic approvals, multi-entity support and deeper integrations. Whether those perks are worth $12 a head is a real question — for a 25-person company it is $3,600 a year, which in our worked example would consume most of the cash value of the points — but the point is that the entry-level version of this ecosystem is genuinely free to run while you find out.

Layered together, the perks tell a consistent story: Brex is priced and designed like software that happens to issue cards, and the perks reward companies that move more of their finance stack into the one system. Which brings us to the flip side.

## Where the value is marketing: five places the brochure bends the truth

None of the following makes Brex a bad product. All of it makes the average rewards summary a bad source of truth. Read these five bends before you sign anything.

**1. The 7x headline describes your smallest category.** Rideshare is the highest multiplier and, for most companies, one of the tiniest lines in the budget. In our worked example it produced 5,600 of 65,800 points — 8.5% of earnings from the category that gets 100% of the marketing. Whenever you see a multiplier, ask "7x of what, exactly, and how much of that do we buy?"

**2. The cash rate is the quiet asterisk.** A program advertised in points is implicitly priced at the best redemption lane. The 0.6-cent cash and statement-credit rate means the program's floor is 40% below its ceiling, and the floor is where buy-now-decide-later teams end up by default.

**3. International spending is a rewards dead zone with a toll.** Purchases processed as international transactions generally earn no points, and when a card transaction requires currency conversion, an FX markup of up to 3% applies. Abroad, then, the card can simultaneously earn nothing and cost up to 3% — the mirror image of the domestic pitch. Companies with heavy foreign spend should model this before anything else.

**4. The best rates live behind behavior changes.** The 4x travel rate requires booking in Brex Travel. The industry schedules require daily repayment, which has a real working-capital cost. The custom rebates require 50+ users and committed spend. Each upgraded number is genuine, and each one is a trade, not a gift.

**5. "No annual fee" is not "no cost."** The card itself has no separate annual fee and Essentials is free, but Premium runs $12 per user per month, travel on paid plans can carry per-trip fees, and FX markups apply on converted transactions. We keep a running breakdown of the full [fees](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-fees) picture precisely because the true cost of a card program is a sum of small lines, not a single headline — and those lines are subtracted from the same pool of value the points flow into.

## Limits: the unglamorous twin of rewards

Rewards conversations usually ignore limits, which is strange, because a generous multiplier on a card you cannot put spend through is worth exactly nothing. Brex sets limits dynamically from your company's financial picture — connected cash balances, revenue, and spending patterns — rather than from a founder's personal credit score, and limits can be materially higher than what a traditional bank offers a young company. The trade-off is that limits move: they rise with your cash position and can tighten when balances fall, and daily-payment products settle so quickly that the practical constraint is liquidity, not the stated limit. Our full explainer on how the [limit](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit) mechanics work is worth reading before you route five figures of monthly SaaS through any card, Brex or otherwise. For the rewards math in this article, the practical takeaway is simple: confirm your limit can actually absorb the spending in your forecast, or your blended earn rate is a fiction.

## Who gets the real value (and who qualifies at all)

Brex underwrites the company, not the founder. In general you need an incorporated U.S. business, an EIN, a U.S. business address and a linked business bank account, and Brex evaluates cash and revenue — funded startups are typically assessed against cash thresholds, while other businesses are assessed on revenue and account history. The complete, current [requirements](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements) checklist is worth reviewing before you apply, because approval is not automatic and the daily-versus-monthly product you are offered shapes the rewards schedule you receive.

Within that qualified pool, the value concentrates in a recognizable profile. The [brex corporate card for startups](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-for-startups) pitch works best for companies that travel regularly, run meaningful SaaS stacks, and have a finance person who will actually look at the dashboard monthly. It works worst for companies whose spend is dominated by one or two giant 1x categories, international purchases, or a team that will redeem everything as statement credit at 0.6 cents and call it a day. Neither group is doing anything wrong — they just have different spending shapes, and rewards programs, all of them, pay for shape rather than effort.

It is also worth saying plainly what kind of product this is: a [brex corporate credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) in the charge-card sense — full repayment, no revolving balance, no personal guarantee. If your company actually needs to carry balances month to month, no rewards analysis can rescue the comparison, because Brex is not selling financing and a rewards point is a terrible reason to change your capital structure.

## Step by step: decide whether Brex value beats a cashback card

Here is the decision procedure, in the order we would run it for a client. It takes about twenty minutes with your last three statements.

**Step 1: Sort last month's card-eligible spend into the five Brex categories.** Rideshare, Brex Travel–style travel, restaurants, recurring software, everything else. Ignore ACH, wires and payroll — they earn nothing on any card program of this type.

**Step 2: Apply your contract's multipliers and total the points.** If you do not have a contract yet, use the standard monthly schedule from this article as the estimate, and stress-test it by moving any doubtful category down to 1x.

**Step 3: Price the points in the lane you will honestly use.** Travel portal user? Multiply by $0.01. Cash person? Multiply by $0.006. Do not let anyone — including us — price your points in a lane you know you will not choose.

**Step 4: Compute the cashback benchmark.** Multiply the same total spend by 2% and by 1.5%. Those are your two bars. Among mainstream [brex business credit cards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) competitors and flat-rate cashback cards alike, this simple range is where most honest comparisons end.

**Step 5: Subtract the frictions.** Deduct the annual cost of any paid plan your team size implies, expected FX markups on foreign spend, and any travel fees. Points are gross; decisions should be made on net.

**Step 6: Read a head-to-head before you commit.** Numbers settle the rewards question, but underwriting fit, limits behavior and software depth settle the relationship. Our independent [review](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-review) scores the full product, and if the math says Brex is not your card, the current [alternatives](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-alternatives) are compared on the same terms — several of them are excellent flat-rate cards that win exactly the comparisons Brex loses.

If Brex wins your Step 3 number against both bars in Step 4, after Step 5's deductions, the rewards case is real and you can stop apologizing for liking the program. If it only wins when priced in a lane you will not use, you have your answer too.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do Brex points expire?** No — points do not expire while your Brex account remains active. There is no use-it-or-lose-it pressure, which is why "we were going to buy this anyway" can be your only redemption rule.

**Is searching "brexbank" going to find me a bank?** It will find you the Brex business account, which is what most people mean: search engines and founders both use [brexbank](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) and "Brex bank" loosely for the account side of the platform — the account that receives your deposits, sends ACH and wires, and anchors your card limits. It is a business account product, and Brex partners with regulated institutions behind the scenes; the day-to-day experience is managed entirely inside Brex.

**Can I earn points on bill pay or wires?** No. Points attach to eligible card transactions only. Bill pay is a control and convenience perk, not an earning channel.

**What is the fastest way to waste Brex points?** Redeeming a large balance as statement credit without checking travel first — it is irreversible and pays 40% less than the travel lane. Second place: transferring points speculatively to an airline program with no specific award in sight.

**Do daily-payment accounts earn the same rewards?** Not necessarily. Daily-payment customers may sit on industry-specific schedules (Advertising, Software, Life Science) with different multipliers, sometimes higher in their core category. Check your contract and the Rewards dashboard; the difference between schedules can be worth more than the difference between cards.

**Is the 7x rideshare rate capped?** Brex's standard program describes its multipliers as uncapped, which is a genuine advantage. Category coding still applies — a purchase must actually code as rideshare.

## The bottom line

The real value of Brex rewards is neither the 7% fantasy of the brochure nor the dismissal you will hear from cashback purists. It is a program with a high, skill-based ceiling — travel redemptions at a full cent, uncapped multipliers, occasional transfer upside — sitting on a modest, honest floor of 0.6 cents in cash. Companies whose spending shape matches the bonus categories, and who redeem with a little discipline, extract value that competes with the best flat-rate cards while getting an expense platform in the bargain. Companies whose spending does not match will quietly earn about 1% and should choose accordingly.

Run your own month through the table before you decide. The arithmetic takes twenty minutes, and unlike a multiplier, it never exaggerates.
