## Slide 5: Second Misconception — One Card, Many Names

- "Brex credit card," "Brex business card," "Brex corporate card" — same product
- Technically it is a **business charge card**, not revolving credit
- Balance is repaid in full — daily or monthly
- No purchase APR, because there is nothing to revolve

![Charge card versus revolving credit card comparison chart](img-02.jpg)

**Speaker notes:** Searchers use half a dozen names — the [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card), the [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card), the [brex corporate credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) — but they all describe one product: the Brex Card. And technically it is a charge card. You cannot carry a balance month to month or make minimum payments. For disciplined companies that is a feature; for businesses that truly need to revolve a balance, it is a deal-breaker, and you should look at conventional [brex business credit cards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) alternatives instead.

## Slide 8: Eligibility — The Identity Requirements

- U.S. Employer Identification Number (EIN)
- Valid U.S. incorporation (C-corp, LLC, and similar entities)
- U.S. operations and a **physical** U.S. address — no P.O. boxes
- Details in our full [requirements guide](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements)

![Checklist of documents needed for a Brex application](img-03.jpg)

**Speaker notes:** Before finances even enter the picture, Brex verifies that the business is real and established in the United States. You will need your EIN, incorporation documents, and a physical U.S. address — a P.O. box or private mailbox service does not satisfy the address requirement. The details you enter must match your incorporation records and bank information exactly, because mismatches between them are the most common cause of verification delays we see.

## Slide 9: Eligibility — The Financial Thresholds

- **Daily payments:** qualify via equity investment or revenue paths (e.g., annual revenue above published thresholds such as $500,000)
- **Monthly payments:** funded startups generally need ~$50,000 minimum cash
- Meeting a threshold never guarantees approval
- No founder personal guarantee; no personal credit check

**Speaker notes:** Brex publishes different financial paths depending on how you will repay. Daily-payment accounts can qualify through investment or revenue tests, while the monthly-payment path for venture-funded startups currently expects a minimum cash balance of around fifty thousand dollars, with some exceptions. Larger companies are assessed on revenue and financial statements. Brex also reviews your business model, source of funds, and expected spending. What it never does is underwrite your personal credit — and it states that applying does not affect your personal credit score.

## Slide 10: Before You Apply — The Preparation Slide

- EIN and incorporation details in one folder
- Physical U.S. address proof (lease, utility bill, or bank statement)
- Beneficial owner and controlling officer ID details
- Recent bank statements or bank-account connection ready
- Funding details (startups) or financial statements (revenue-based)

**Speaker notes:** Applications stall when applicants go hunting for documents mid-flow. Spend fifteen minutes preparing: your EIN confirmation, incorporation paperwork, address evidence, identification details for beneficial owners and the controlling officer, and either access to connect your business bank account or recent financial statements. If you have raised capital, have the amounts and investor names handy. This preparation step is the cheapest way to turn a multi-day application into a same-day one.

## Slide 12: The Application — Steps 4 to 6

- **Step 4:** Enter legal company details exactly as incorporated
- **Step 5:** Complete identity and compliance (KYC) verification
- **Step 6:** Connect your bank or submit financial statements

![Founder connecting a business bank account during onboarding](img-04.jpg)

**Speaker notes:** Step four asks for the legal business name, EIN, entity type, state of incorporation, industry, address, and a plain description of what the company does. Step five is the regulatory Know-Your-Customer check on the business and its owners — it is a compliance verification, not a personal credit pull. Step six gives Brex the financial visibility it uses to set your limit: most applicants connect their primary business bank account in the flow, while larger applicants upload statements instead.

## Slide 13: The Application — Steps 7 to 10

- **Step 7:** Accept your repayment schedule (daily or monthly)
- **Step 8:** Wait for the underwriting decision
- **Step 9:** Configure budgets, approvals, and receipt rules first
- **Step 10:** Issue physical and virtual cards to the team

**Speaker notes:** Step seven confirms how you repay: daily collection or a monthly statement paid in full. Step eight is Brex's underwriting review — straightforward, well-documented applications are typically decided quickly. Step nine is where disciplined companies separate themselves: set spend limits, approval chains, and receipt policies *before* issuing a single card. Only then, step ten, do you issue physical cards for in-person spending and virtual cards for subscriptions and vendors, each capped by its own limit.

## Slide 15: Three Card Types, One System

- **Employee cards** — everyday staff spending, individual limits
- **Purchasing cards (P-cards)** — vendor and operational spend
- **Virtual cards** — instant, purpose-specific numbers for subscriptions

**Speaker notes:** Every [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card) lives in the same system but plays a different role. Employee cards handle travel, meals, and supplies. Purchasing cards carry vendor spending — SaaS, cloud infrastructure, advertising — so a subscription survives staff changes and a compromised vendor card can be replaced without touching anyone else. Virtual cards are created instantly for one purpose and one budget, which is the cleanest way to isolate recurring charges and spot forgotten subscriptions at renewal time.

## Slide 16: How Brex Calculates Your Limit

- Company limit = ceiling; card limits divide it up
- Based on: bank balances, Brex account funds, revenue, capital raised, financials
- Daily-payment limits track account funds closely
- Limits move as your financial profile moves

**Speaker notes:** Your company limit is the ceiling for total card spend, and individual card and budget limits decide how much of that ceiling any person or purpose can use. Brex calculates the ceiling from business-level data — connected bank balances, funds held in the Brex business account, revenue, funding, and statements. Be aware that the limit is dynamic: a growing cash position can raise it, and a shrinking one can lower it. Our [brex card limit](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit) guide walks through the mechanics in detail.

## Slide 18: Fees — What It Actually Costs

- Card annual fee: **$0**
- Essentials plan: $0 per user/month
- Premium plan: $12 per user/month
- FX markup on currency conversion: up to 3%
- No interest — but no revolving either; full payment is mandatory

**Speaker notes:** The pricing story is mostly about software, not the card. The card itself carries no annual fee, and the free Essentials tier covers simpler teams, while Premium at twelve dollars per user per month unlocks advanced controls. The costs to watch are the foreign-exchange markup — up to three percent when a currency conversion is required — and the discipline cost: because the balance must be paid in full, missing the schedule is not an option. ## Slide 19: Expense Management — Where Brex Pulls Away

- Policies enforced before purchase via spend limits
- Receipts auto-collected and matched; AI-assisted categorization
- Exceptions routed for approval, not discovered weeks later
- Reimbursements, Bill Pay, and travel in the same platform
- Syncs coded transactions to your accounting software

![Expense approval workflow from purchase to accounting sync](img-07.jpg)

**Speaker notes:** A traditional card hands you a statement at month-end and leaves the detective work to you. Brex inverts that: limits and policies are set in advance, receipts are captured at the point of purchase, out-of-policy items are flagged immediately, and coded transactions flow into accounting. Bill Pay handles invoices by ACH, check, wire, or card, and travel bookings tie back to travel budgets. This is why Brex is best understood as finance software with a card attached — not a card with an app attached.

## Slide 22: Cons — The Case for Caution

- Charge card only: full payment required, no revolving
- Sole proprietors and unincorporated businesses excluded
- Cash/revenue thresholds shut out many very small companies
- FX markup adds up for heavy international spenders
- Advanced features sit behind the paid per-user plan

![Balanced scale weighing Brex pros and cons](img-08.jpg)

**Speaker notes:** The limitations are structural, not incidental. A company that needs to revolve balances cannot use Brex as designed. The eligibility thresholds — incorporation, cash, or revenue — deliberately exclude the smallest and earliest businesses, whatever their potential. International teams should model the currency-conversion markup on their real spending before committing. And while the free tier is genuine, larger teams with complex approval chains should budget for the Premium per-user pricing from the start rather than being surprised by it later.
