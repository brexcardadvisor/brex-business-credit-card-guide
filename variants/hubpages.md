## Requirement Numbers: The Thresholds You Must Clear

Brex publishes its eligibility in fairly concrete terms, which makes the first calculation easy: either your company clears the bars or it does not. The full detail lives on our [requirements page](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements), but the essentials are:

**Identity and structure requirements**
- A U.S. Employer Identification Number (EIN) issued by the IRS
- U.S. incorporation — the company must be an incorporated entity (C-corp, LLC and similar)
- U.S. operations and a physical U.S. address (a P.O. box or private mailbox address does not qualify)
- Sole proprietors and unincorporated partnerships are **not** eligible — this is a hard rule, not a guideline

**Financial requirements**
- For monthly-payment accounts, funded startups generally need a minimum cash balance of around $50,000, with some exceptions depending on the funding profile
- Revenue-based companies are assessed on revenue and financial statements rather than a single cash figure
- Brex also reviews business model, source of funds and expected spending patterns — meeting a published threshold starts the conversation, it does not guarantee approval

Notice what is missing from that list: any reference to the founder's personal credit history. A founder with a mediocre personal score but a well-funded company can qualify; a founder with a perfect personal score but an unincorporated side project cannot. The [brex corporate card for startups](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-for-startups) was built around exactly that inversion.

## Limit Numbers: How Brex Decides What You Can Spend

The most-asked question about any [brex corporate card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card) is "how big will my limit be?" Brex does not publish a fixed tier table, because the limit is calculated from your company's financial data: connected bank balances, funds held in the Brex business account, revenue, capital raised and, for larger companies, financial statements. Our [guide to Brex card limits](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit) explains the mechanics in depth; here, let us run two worked examples to show how differently the calculation plays out.

### Worked Example 1: The Funded Startup

Imagine a Delaware C-corp, eighteen months old, that raised a seed round and keeps **$200,000** in its operating account. It has ten employees and monthly card spending needs of roughly $35,000 across cloud services, ads and travel.

- Under a traditional issuer, the limit would be anchored to the founder's personal credit — often a four- or low-five-figure line that the company outgrows immediately.
- Under Brex's model, the underwriting input is the $200,000 cash position and the funding behind it. The company clears the roughly $50,000 threshold for monthly payments with room to spare, and its overall company limit is set from its financial profile rather than anyone's personal file.
- Inside that company limit, the finance lead might set a $5,000 monthly limit on each employee card, a $15,000 purchasing card for the engineering team's cloud bills, and virtual cards capped per subscription — the company ceiling and the individual ceilings are separate numbers, managed separately.

### Worked Example 2: The Revenue-Based Company

Now imagine a bootstrapped software company, no venture funding, with **$1.2 million in annual revenue** and about $60,000 in average cash. It has twenty-five employees.

- There is no big cash pile to underwrite against, so Brex's review leans on revenue and financial statements instead.
- The company can still qualify — the funded-startup cash threshold is one path, not the only path — but its limit will track its demonstrated revenue and cash flow rather than a bank balance.
- If this company instead chose daily payments, its spending capacity would be tied much more tightly to funds held in the Brex business account day by day, because daily-payment limits are primarily linked to that account balance.

### The Two Limit Numbers That Actually Matter

| Number | What it controls | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| Company limit | Total card spending across the whole business | Brex, from your financials |
| Card / spend limit | What one person, team or purpose can spend | You, inside the company limit |

The practical takeaway: when you compare a [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card) against a bank card, compare the *usable* number — the limit your team can actually spend against without a founder's personal guarantee capping it — not the marketing number.

## FX Numbers: The Markup Math on International Spending

Brex cards can be used internationally, but when a transaction requires a currency conversion, Brex applies a foreign-exchange markup of **up to 3%**. That single percentage is the most commonly overlooked number in the whole product, so let us price it on realistic spends:

| Monthly foreign-currency spend | At 1% markup | At 3% markup | Annual cost at 3% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2,000 | $20/mo | $60/mo | $720/yr |
| $10,000 | $100/mo | $300/mo | $3,600/yr |
| $50,000 | $500/mo | $1,500/mo | $18,000/yr |

The lesson is not that Brex is expensive abroad — percentage markups in this range are common across business cards, including many that advertise "no foreign transaction fee" while baking the margin into the conversion rate. The lesson is that the FX line belongs in your comparison spreadsheet. A company spending $50,000 a month in foreign currencies should weigh that up-to-$18,000 annual figure against everything else in this article; a company whose international spend is a few SaaS subscriptions billed in euros will never notice it.

Also note the cost that is *absent*: because the [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) is a charge card repaid in full, there is no interest cost column at all. Compare that with a revolving card carrying a balance at a typical business APR and the interest line can dwarf every fee discussed here — but the flip side is real too: Brex gives you no option to spread a large purchase over several months. The number is $0 in interest precisely because borrowing is not on the menu.

## Rewards Numbers: What Points Are Worth in Practice

Brex runs a points program on card spending: a base earn rate, with category multipliers available under its rewards programs for common business categories such as rideshare, travel booked through its platform, restaurants and software. Points can be redeemed in business-friendly ways — toward travel, statement value or transfers to partner programs, depending on your plan and the current program terms. Our [rewards guide](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards) covers the current structure; here is how to value it with your own numbers.

**Step 1: Estimate your annual eligible spend.** Say your company puts $40,000 a month — $480,000 a year — through its cards.

**Step 2: Split it into multiplier and base categories.** Suppose half falls in multiplier categories and half earns the base rate. Your effective blended earn rate is what matters, not the headline multiplier.

**Step 3: Value the points conservatively.** Value each point at one cent unless you have a specific higher-value redemption (such as a favorable travel transfer) that you will genuinely use. Optimistic per-point valuations are how rewards math goes wrong.

**Step 4: Compare against the alternative.** A flat cashback card returning, say, 1.5% on $480,000 returns $7,200 a year with zero effort. Your Brex rewards need to clear roughly that bar — after accounting for which categories actually earn multipliers — before rewards alone justify the switch.

For most companies, they will not be the deciding factor, and that is fine, because of the next section.

## The "Value" Number: Why Points Are Only Half the Equation

When finance teams discuss [brex value](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards), they are usually adding up a different column entirely:

- **Hours not spent chasing receipts**, because documentation is captured at the point of purchase and matched automatically
- **Out-of-policy spend that never happens**, because limits and policies are enforced before the transaction, not discovered after it
- **Duplicate and zombie subscriptions caught early**, because each vendor sits on a visible, dedicated virtual card
- **A limit based on your company's actual cash position**, instead of a ceiling set by a founder's personal credit file
- **Business credit building**, because Brex reports payment performance to business credit bureaus

None of those appear in a points calculator, yet for a 40-person company they routinely outweigh the rewards line. The honest summary: judge [brex business credit cards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) like this one as spend-management software with a card attached, and let the rewards be a tiebreaker, not the headline.

## Step by Step: Applying for Brex, in Order

Numbers checked and decision made? Here is the application process as a numbered sequence, in the order you will actually do it.

1. **Run the eligibility check against your own numbers.** Confirm U.S. incorporation, an EIN, a physical U.S. address, and which financial path you fit — funded startup with around $50,000+ in cash for monthly payments, or a revenue-based profile. Sole proprietors should stop here and look at the alternatives below.
2. **Assemble the documents in one folder.** EIN and incorporation details, business address evidence, beneficial-owner and controlling-officer identification, recent bank statements, and either funding details or financial statements depending on your path.
3. **Start the application on the Brex website.** Create the admin account with a long-term work email — this becomes your everyday [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login), so do not use a disposable address.
4. **Enter company information exactly as registered.** Legal name, EIN, entity type and state, industry and address must match your incorporation and banking records; mismatches are the most common cause of verification delays.
5. **Complete identity and compliance verification.** Owners and the controlling officer provide identification details for the regulatory KYC check. This is not a personal credit check and does not affect personal credit scores.
6. **Connect your bank or upload financials.** This data is what your opening limit is calculated from, so connect the primary operating account, not a side account with a flattering or misleading balance.
7. **Confirm your repayment schedule.** Daily or monthly, based on how your account qualified. Either way the balance is paid in full — set a calendar reminder for the monthly date if that is your schedule.
8. **Wait for underwriting, and answer requests completely.** If Brex asks for an extra document, send exactly that document; partial responses are the most common self-inflicted delay.
9. **Configure before you issue a single card.** Create team budgets and spend limits, set approval policies and receipt rules, invite the finance team with the right roles, and connect your accounting software.
10. **Issue cards and set them loose.** Physical cards for in-person spending, virtual cards for subscriptions and vendors — each capped by its own spend limit inside the company limit Brex set.

## Quick Answers to the Questions the Numbers Raise

**How much cash do I need to qualify?** For the classic startup path to monthly payments, around $50,000. Revenue-based companies are judged on revenue and statements instead, and daily-payment accounts are tied to funds held in the Brex business account.

**What will my limit be?** There is no published fixed figure — it is calculated from your connected balances, account funds, revenue, funding and financials, and it can move as those numbers move. Expect it to reflect your company's scale rather than your personal credit.

**What does Brex cost in total?** $0 annual card fee, plus $0 per user per month on Essentials or $12 per user per month on Premium, plus an FX markup of up to 3% on currency conversions. No interest, because there is no revolving balance.

**Is the single-word "brexcard" search the same product as the corporate card?** Yes — the [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card), the charge card, and the corporate card are one product described three ways.

## The Verdict, in Numbers

Strip away the branding and the decision is a short checklist. Brex fits if your company is incorporated in the U.S. with an EIN and a physical address, holds meaningful cash (around $50,000 or more on the startup path) or generates real revenue, employs people who need to spend, and can pay its balance in full every cycle. In return you get a limit calculated from your company's finances, no personal guarantee, $0 in card annual fees, software that runs from $0 to $12 per user per month, and controls that prevent bad spending instead of reporting it.

It does not fit if you are a sole proprietor, if your business genuinely needs to carry balances month to month, or if your foreign-currency spending is so large that an up-to-3% conversion markup outweighs everything else in the table. For those companies, the alternatives will serve better today — and Brex will still be there, with the same numbers, when the company grows into them.

Run your own figures through the five tables in this guide before you apply. If the math works on paper, the ten steps above will get you from application to a working card program without surprises.
