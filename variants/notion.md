## 1. Purpose

This document defines exactly how our company applies for, configures, issues, uses, and reconciles the [brex corporate card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card). The goal is simple: every dollar of card spend should be approved before it happens, documented when it happens, and reconciled without detective work at month-end.

Before this SOP, card spend ran on a shared bank card, screenshots of receipts in chat threads, and a founder's personal limit absorbing company ad spend. That model breaks the moment a team grows past a handful of people. A structured [brex business credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) program replaces it with per-person cards, per-purpose budgets, and an audit trail that finance can actually trust.

This procedure covers the full lifecycle:

1. Confirming the company is eligible
2. Submitting the application
3. Configuring the account before any card is issued
4. Issuing physical and virtual cards
5. Setting limits, budgets, and approval rules
6. Day-to-day purchase and receipt handling
7. Weekly review and month-end close
8. Handling exceptions, lost cards, and offboarding

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to:

- **In scope:** All employee cards, purchasing cards (P-cards), and virtual cards issued under the company Brex account; all spend made through the Brex business account; reimbursements submitted through the same platform.
- **Out of scope:** Personal cards (never to be used for company spend except in a documented emergency), petty cash, and vendor payments made by wire outside the platform, which follow the AP (accounts payable) procedure instead.

Any employee who holds a card, approves spend, or codes transactions is bound by this procedure. Managers are responsible for making sure their direct reports have read it before a card is activated in their name.

## 3. Background: What Brex Is (and Is Not)

New joiners regularly ask why the company uses Brex instead of a normal bank card, so the SOP records the answer once, here.

[Brex](https://brexcardadvisor.com) is a financial technology company founded in 2017 in San Francisco. The [brex company](https://brexcardadvisor.com) was built around a different underwriting idea: instead of judging a founder's personal credit history, Brex judges the business — its cash balance, revenue, funding, and spending patterns. In January 2026, Capital One announced its agreement to acquire Brex, and the acquisition was completed on April 7, 2026. Brex continues to operate under its own brand, now as part of Capital One.

Three facts every cardholder should understand:

1. **The Brex Card is a charge card, not a revolving credit card.** People call it the [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) or the [brex corporate credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-corporate-card), but technically the balance is repaid in full on a set schedule — daily or monthly, depending on how the account qualifies. There is no option to carry a balance and pay interest.
2. **There is no personal guarantee and no personal credit check.** Applying does not affect any employee's or founder's personal credit score, because underwriting is done at company level.
3. **Brex is not a bank.** New staff often search for [brex bank](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) or even the merged term brexbank when they first hear about the account. Brex is a fintech company, not a bank; its business account services are delivered through partner arrangements. This matters for how we describe the account in vendor paperwork — never list Brex as the company's bank of record without checking with Finance first.

People reach the platform through many routes — typing [brex com](https://brexcardadvisor.com) into a browser, searching the single word [brexcard](https://brexcardadvisor.com), or following a recommendation. However a cardholder arrives, the procedures below are the same.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

A card program fails when everyone assumes someone else is watching. This SOP names the watchers.

### 4.1 Account Admin (Finance Lead)

- Owns the Brex account structure: budgets, spend limits, approval policies, receipt rules
- Approves all new card requests and limit increases above the standard tier
- Reviews the exceptions queue weekly
- Owns this SOP and its quarterly review

### 4.2 Bookkeeper / Finance Operations

- Codes transactions to the correct chart of accounts daily or every other day
- Chases missing receipts and memos using the escalation ladder in Section 11
- Runs the month-end close checklist in Section 12
- Maintains the accounting integration and resolves sync errors

### 4.3 Cardholding Employee

- Spends only within the limit and policy attached to their card
- Attaches a receipt and writes a business-purpose memo at the time of purchase
- Never shares a card number; uses a dedicated virtual card for subscriptions instead
- Reports a lost card or suspicious transaction the same day

### 4.4 Approving Manager

- Approves or rejects out-of-policy and over-threshold expenses within two business days
- Confirms that approved spend has a genuine business purpose
- Requests limit changes for their team through the Admin, not directly

## 5. Eligibility: Confirm the Company Qualifies Before Applying

Do not start the application until every item in this checklist is confirmed. The full detail lives on our [requirements page](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-requirements); the operational summary is:

**Business identity checklist**

- [ ] U.S. Employer Identification Number (EIN) issued by the IRS
- [ ] Valid U.S. incorporation (C-corp, LLC, or another incorporated entity)
- [ ] U.S. operations and a physical U.S. street address (a P.O. box or private mailbox does not count)
- [ ] The business is **not** a sole proprietorship or unincorporated partnership — these are not eligible

**Financial checklist (path depends on repayment type)**

- [ ] *Daily payments path:* the company meets the published profile based on factors such as equity investment, or annual revenue above Brex's current thresholds (one common published path is annual revenue above $500,000)
- [ ] *Monthly payments path:* funded startups generally hold a minimum cash balance — Brex currently publishes $50,000 for venture-funded startups, with some exceptions — while larger companies are assessed on revenue and financial statements

Meeting a threshold never guarantees approval. Brex also reviews the business model, source of funds, and expected spending patterns. The classic fit is the [brex corporate card for startups](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-for-startups) profile — incorporated, funded, and spending from day one — but revenue-generating companies qualify on the commercial path as well.

**Stop condition:** If the company is a sole proprietorship, stop here. Record the decision in the finance tracker and evaluate traditional [brex business credit cards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) alternatives instead (see Section 15).

## 6. Procedure A: Submitting the Application

**Owner:** Finance Lead  **Estimated time:** 30–45 minutes, plus review time

1. Open the Brex website and start the signup flow. Create the admin account with a company-controlled email address (for example, finance@yourcompany.com) — never a personal inbox, because this account becomes the master login for the whole program.
2. Set a strong, unique password and store it in the company password manager. This is the credential used at every future [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login).
3. Enter the legal company name, EIN, incorporation type and state, industry, and physical business address. These details must match incorporation records and bank information character-for-character; mismatches are the most common cause of verification delays.
4. Write the business description field carefully: what the company sells, to whom, and how it makes money. Vague descriptions trigger follow-up questions.
5. Complete identity verification for beneficial owners and the controlling officer. Each person provides identification details for the regulatory Know-Your-Customer check. Reassure anyone who asks: this is a compliance check, not a personal credit pull.
6. Connect the primary business bank account through the secure connection flow, or upload financial statements if applying on the monthly-payments path (income statement, balance sheet, cash-flow statement).
7. Select the repayment schedule Brex offers based on qualification — daily or monthly. Record the decision and its cash-flow implication in the finance tracker.
8. Submit and monitor the application inbox daily. If Brex requests additional documents, respond with exactly what was requested within one business day.
9. When approval arrives, do **not** issue cards yet. Proceed to Procedure B first.

## 7. Procedure B: Configuring the Account Before Issuing Cards

**Owner:** Admin  **Rule:** No card leaves this section unconfigured. Controls are the entire point of the platform; a [brex business card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) issued without limits is just a faster way to lose track of money.

1. **Create the budget structure.** Mirror the chart of accounts: one budget per department (Engineering, Sales, Marketing, Operations, G&A), plus purpose budgets for Travel and Software where they cut across teams.
2. **Set approval policies.** Define thresholds, for example: purchases under $100 auto-approved within limit; $100–$1,000 require manager approval; above $1,000 require Finance Lead approval. Encode them in the platform, not in a wiki page nobody reads.
3. **Set receipt and memo rules.** Require a receipt for every transaction above the company's documentation threshold and a memo on every transaction, no exceptions by amount for card-present purchases like travel.
4. **Invite users and assign roles.** Admin (Finance only), Bookkeeper, Approver (managers), Employee (cardholders). Apply least-privilege: nobody gets Admin "just in case."
5. **Connect the accounting software.** Map Brex categories to the chart of accounts, set the sync schedule, and run one test transaction end-to-end before rollout.
6. **Enable security defaults.** Require two-factor authentication for all users; enable single sign-on if the company identity provider supports it.
7. **Fund or link the payment source.** For daily-payments accounts, confirm the Brex business account balance process — daily limits are tied closely to funds held there (see Section 10).

## 8. Procedure C: Issuing Cards

**Owner:** Admin  **Trigger:** Approved card request from a manager

1. Confirm the request: employee name, card type, budget, and monthly limit.
2. Choose the card type:
   - **Physical employee card** — for in-person spend: travel, meals, office purchases.
   - **Virtual card** — for online purchases and subscriptions. Rule: every recurring vendor gets its *own* virtual card, so a cancelled or compromised number never takes down ten subscriptions at once.
   - **Purchasing card (P-card)** — for departmental/vendor spend such as cloud services and ad platforms, owned by a role rather than a person where the platform allows.
3. Attach the card to the correct budget and set the cardholder limit — an employee's card limit is always a *subset* of the company limit, never the whole line.
4. Set the card's start conditions: temporary limits for travel (for example, a conference window) should carry an expiry date at creation time, not be cleaned up later.
5. Notify the cardholder and link them to Sections 10 and 11 of this SOP. A card is not considered "issued" until the holder has acknowledged the receipt and memo rules.
6. Cardholder activates the card on receipt and adds virtual cards to a mobile wallet where supported.
7. Log the issuance in the card register: holder, last four digits, budget, limit, date.

## 9. Procedure D: Limits — How They Work and How to Change Them

Two layers of limits govern every purchase:

- **Company limit:** the ceiling for total card spending, set by Brex from company financials — connected bank balances, funds in the business account, revenue, capital raised, and financial statements.
- **Card and budget limits:** the company's own subdivisions of that ceiling, controlled by the Admin.

Our detailed explainer on the [brex card limit](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-limit) covers the calculation; the operational rules are:

1. Limits are reviewed monthly by the Admin against actual utilisation. A team consistently above 80% utilisation gets a review, not an automatic increase.
2. Temporary increases (events, annual software renewals, inventory buys) are requested by the manager with a business reason and an end date, approved by the Finance Lead, and set to expire automatically.
3. Decreases take effect immediately and need no approval — any Admin may cut a limit the moment fraud or misuse is suspected.
4. Remember that limits can move at Brex's side too: a shrinking cash position can reduce the company limit, so Finance checks the available limit before any large planned purchase.

## 10. The Business Account in Daily Operations

The [brex business](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-account) account sits at the centre of the platform: funds are held there, card repayments are drawn from there, and — for daily-payments accounts — the balance effectively drives how much the team can spend that day.

Operating rules:

1. Finance Operations checks the account balance every morning before 10:00 local time. On a daily-payments account, a low balance silently shrinks everyone's spending power.
2. Maintain a buffer of at least five business days of average card spend above expected daily debits.
3. All inbound customer payments that the company routes to this account are reconciled the same day by the Bookkeeper.
4. Never describe this account externally as a bank account. In banking-detail forms that require a bank name, escalate to the Finance Lead rather than improvising.

## 11. Procedure E: Day-to-Day Use — Purchases, Receipts, Memos

**Owner:** Every cardholder

1. **Before you buy:** confirm the purchase fits your card's budget and limit. If it needs approval under Section 7 thresholds, get the approval first — approvals after a purchase are exceptions, and exceptions are counted.
2. **At purchase:** pay with the correct card. Subscriptions go on their dedicated virtual card, never on a personal or shared card.
3. **Immediately after purchase:** photograph or forward the receipt and attach it to the transaction; write a memo that states the business purpose in plain words ("Q3 design software renewal — Figma, 12 seats"). "Stuff" is not a memo.
4. **Foreign currency spend:** permitted for travel and international vendors. Note that currency conversion carries an FX markup of up to 3%, so route very large foreign purchases through Finance first to confirm the cheapest payment route.
5. **Prohibited:** personal purchases (even if you intend to repay), cash advances, splitting a purchase to dodge an approval threshold, and lending your card or number to anyone — including your manager.

### Receipt escalation ladder (Bookkeeper)

1. Day 2 after transaction: automated reminder to the cardholder.
2. Day 5: direct message from the Bookkeeper naming the missing items.
3. Day 10: manager copied; card flagged in the weekly exceptions report.
4. Day 15: card suspended until documentation is complete. Reinstatement is by the Admin only.

## 12. Procedure F: Brex Login and Access Security

Every user reaches the platform through the standard sign-in. Our reference guide for the [brex login](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-login) process covers the screens; this SOP sets the security standards around it:

1. Two-factor authentication is mandatory for every user, without exception. New accounts are not considered active until 2FA is enabled.
2. Passwords live in the company password manager. Sharing the admin login is a dismissible offence under the IT policy.
3. Use single sign-on where available so that offboarding in the identity provider automatically cuts platform access.
4. Employees use the mobile app for receipts and approvals; admins perform configuration from the web dashboard, where the full audit log is visible.
5. **Offboarding procedure (same business day as departure):** Admin freezes the leaver's cards, reassigns any vendor virtual cards they owned to their manager, revokes their platform access, and the Bookkeeper confirms all of their transactions have receipts and memos before final payroll.

## 13. Procedure G: Weekly Review and Month-End Close

### Weekly review (Bookkeeper + Admin, 30 minutes)

- [ ] Exceptions queue cleared or escalated
- [ ] New transactions coded to the correct accounts
- [ ] Any declined transactions investigated (limit? policy? suspected fraud?)
- [ ] Upcoming renewals on virtual cards confirmed with budget owners

### Month-end close checklist

1. **Day 1:** Freeze the period. Notify all cardholders that receipts for the closing month are due by end of day.
2. **Day 1–2:** Run the escalation ladder for every missing receipt or memo. No transaction crosses into the new month undocumented without the Finance Lead's written exception.
3. **Day 2:** Verify the statement balance against the dashboard total. Because the [brex card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card) is a charge card, the full statement amount must be paid on schedule — confirm the funding account holds enough cash and diary the debit.
4. **Day 3:** Sync all coded transactions to the accounting software; resolve every sync error the same day.
5. **Day 3:** Reconcile the card liability account to zero after the statement payment clears.
6. **Day 4:** Produce the spend report by budget vs. plan; flag any budget more than 10% over plan to its owner with a written explanation request.
7. **Day 5:** Finance Lead signs off the close, archives the report with this SOP's records, and logs any policy breaches for the quarterly review.

## 15. Rewards and Total Value

Card spend earns points under Brex's rewards programs, with category multipliers in common business categories and redemption options such as travel or statement value depending on plan and current terms — details are tracked on our [brex card rewards](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards) page.

This SOP's position: **points are a by-product, never a purchasing reason.** When Finance evaluates [brex value](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-rewards), it counts the operational savings first — hours not spent chasing receipts, out-of-policy spend prevented by limits, duplicate subscriptions caught because each vendor sits on a visible dedicated card — and treats points as a small rebate on top. No employee may choose a more expensive vendor or fare class to earn more points. Ever.

## 16. When Brex Is Not the Right Tool

An honest SOP names its own limits. Escalate to the Finance Lead for an alternatives review when:

- The company needs to **carry a balance** across months — a charge card cannot do this; a traditional revolving business card can. Our comparison of [brex alternatives](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-alternatives) maps the options.
- A new entity is a **sole proprietorship** — it cannot be onboarded (Section 5).
- A team's spend is so small that platform overhead exceeds the benefit; a single simple card may be rational.
- FX-heavy spend makes the conversion markup material versus a specialist provider.

Our overall assessment, for the record, is in the full [brex card review](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-card-review): for incorporated, growing companies that pay in full, this remains one of the strongest programs available in 2026 — which is why it is the standard this document describes.

## 17. Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this a credit card or a charge card?**
A charge card. The balance is repaid in full, daily or monthly. Anyone searching for a [brex credit card](https://brexcardadvisor.com/brex-business-credit-card) is describing this same product.

**Will applying affect my personal credit score?**
No. Underwriting is at company level, there is no personal guarantee, and Brex states applying does not affect personal credit.

**I joined last week. How do I sign in?**
Use your company email and the password you set at invitation, via the normal login page or the mobile app, and enable two-factor authentication immediately (Section 12).

**Can I use my card for a personal purchase and repay it?**
No. Personal spend on a company card is a policy breach even if repaid (Section 11).

**A vendor needs banking details and asks for our bank's name. What do I write?**
Stop and ask the Finance Lead. Brex is a fintech platform, not a bank (Section 3), and the correct wording depends on the partner arrangement behind the account.

**My limit is too low for a legitimate purchase. What is the fastest route?**
Manager request → Finance Lead approval → Admin sets a temporary increase with an expiry date (Section 9). Plan renewals a week ahead; "urgent" limit requests are a planning failure, and the exceptions report counts them.

**We are a startup that just raised. Is this program built for us?**
Yes — the funded-startup path is the original design centre of the product, provided the cash threshold for monthly payments is met (Section 5).
